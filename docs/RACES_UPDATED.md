# Otherreach - Playable Races

**Repository codename:** UNNAMED

Companion documents:

| Document | Covers |
|---|---|
| **this file** | mechanics: aptitudes, balance, coverage, visual/morphological identity |
| `MYTHOLOGY.md` | cosmology, origins, beliefs, cultures, NPC and hireling use |
| `STONE_QUESTION.md` | the Kal / Ondrek conflict and its questline |

All eight races are written for eventual play. For the vertical slice, implement a
subset as player options and keep the rest as NPC cultures — the material exists either
way, so nothing needs inventing later.

Design frame: every race is defined by **its relationship to Pacha** — a complete
reality of space, time, matter, history and physical law. Native to this one, migrant
from another, artificial, or from outside every Pacha. That single axis produces the
biology, the aptitudes, and the frictions between them.

Nothing here is a stock fantasy race. There are no dwarves, elves or gnomes.

---

## Naming principle

One ancestral root family appears in the oldest layer of every language: three
syllables, **veth**, **kal**, **mor**. Each culture kept one and lost the others, and
each now believes its syllable is the original word for *person*.

That is the quiet clue: the human-like races are one lineage, scattered by an earlier
Pachakuti, and they have been telling themselves they are unrelated for thousands of
years. The reveal is not a new taxonomy — it is that the word they already use for
themselves answers the question nobody asked.

---

## Visual and morphology design principle

Shared ancestry gives the setting a believable reason for several peoples to retain a
broadly compatible body plan: upright posture, one head, two arms, two legs, similar
joint placement, similar atmosphere and similar basic metabolic needs. That does **not**
mean they should read as humans wearing different skins.

The target is:

> **Shared body-plan ancestry, distinct silhouettes and distinct biology.**

At normal gameplay distance, each major race should be identifiable from posture,
proportion, movement and silhouette wherever practical. Skin color, hair and clothing
should reinforce identity, not carry it alone.

A simple production test is the **solid-black silhouette test**: if two major races in
neutral poses are difficult to distinguish at gameplay distance, their proportions,
posture or morphology probably need another pass.

Production practicality still matters. Most races should reuse a small number of
compatible skeleton, animation and armor-fit families. Exceptions are intentional:
the Mor are not conventionally physical, and the Constructed can support deliberate
modular morphology.

The visual target is therefore not “every species needs a new pelvis.” It is “every
species should visibly belong to its own history.”

## Biology, aptitude and culture are separate

Future character creation and progression must distinguish three different sources of
racial identity:

### Innate biology

Traits physically or metaphysically built into the body:

- unusual joints;
- wings;
- sensory structures;
- material composition;
- ethereal existence;
- true inability to interface with a form of magic.

These cannot normally be learned merely by spending skill points.

### Inherited aptitude

A biological or metaphysical tendency that makes some disciplines easier, deeper or
more intuitive.

An aptitude is an advantage, not automatically an exclusive monopoly.

### Cultural / technical knowledge

What a civilization teaches:

- engineering;
- magical theory;
- combat traditions;
- scientific knowledge;
- languages;
- rituals;
- crafting methods.

This is **learned knowledge**, not genetic memory. A Vaskaal infant is not born knowing
how to repair a starship. A human raised among Vaskaal may understand their technology
better than a Vaskaal raised entirely in Otherhome.

This distinction is important for mixed lineage, adoption, teachers, cross-cultural
skill acquisition and the long-term skill system.

## 1. Veth — the touched lineage

**Aptitude: melee, ranged. Balance: martial.**

Ordinary humans, insofar as that word means anything here. What distinguishes them is
heritage: their ancestors survived a Pachakuti. Reality-adjacent blood makes them
learn faster than they should and grasp unfamiliar skills in weeks.

The cost is a ceiling. The touched reach competence with startling speed, then stall.
The best swordsman alive is not Veth.

Culturally they are the majority and the least examined — the ones who never had to
think about what a Pacha is, because they have never been outside one.

### Visual identity

The Veth are the familiar visual anchor of the roster, but close observation should
reveal that their bodies are subtly too adaptable to be ordinary Earth humans.

Typical traits include:

- slightly elongated forearms and lower legs;
- long fingers and toes;
- unusually mobile shoulders, hips, wrists and ankles;
- joint ranges that make some positions look mildly dislocated to other races;
- a slightly narrower ribcage and longer overall line;
- unusually reflective irises.

The effect should be uncanny only after movement draws attention to it. A Veth climbing
a wall, changing stance or slipping through a narrow opening may bend in ways a human
observer instinctively expects to hurt.

This physical plasticity mirrors their broader identity as the lineage that adapts
quickly after contact with the unfamiliar.

**Mechanically:** fastest skill acquisition, lowest skill cap, no innate resource.
The reliable all-rounder and the race most players will understand immediately.

Possible minor traversal implications from their unusual flexibility should remain
secondary to their core learning mechanic and should not become a new racial role lock.

## 2. Kal — the calcified

**Aptitude: science, crafting. Balance: slow, durable, precise.**

Broad, short, dense — built for cold and for work rather than for running. They think
in materials and structure the way other races think in words, and their work outlasts
them by centuries.

### Visual identity

The Kal should not read as conventional ogres or dwarves. Their most immediately
surprising anatomical feature is a pair of **large folding dorsal wings**.

At rest, the wing spars collapse tightly along channels beside the spine and can be
partially concealed by cloaks, packs or specially fitted armor. When deployed, their
span appears absurdly large for such a compact, dense body.

The wings are functional, but the Kal are poorly matched to sustained flight under the
present world's conditions, particularly while armored or carrying heavy equipment.
Their normal uses are more likely to include:

- controlled descent;
- braking;
- stabilization;
- jump assistance;
- crossing short gaps;
- brief bursts of lift under favorable conditions.

A lightly equipped and highly trained Kal may be capable of more, but unrestricted
flight is not the baseline racial capability.

One possible ancestral explanation is that the Kal evolved in a Pacha with lower
gravity, denser atmosphere, or both. That explanation is a strong working possibility,
not yet a required cosmological fact.

Kal armor uses dorsal wing channels, articulated back plates or equivalent clearances
rather than requiring a wholly separate armor system.

Their biology may also have influenced ancestral architecture: vertical foundries,
landing shelves, cliff structures, roof access and balconies could survive as cultural
forms even where modern gravity makes wing use less practical.

Their tragedy is Pacha transitions. Where a Veth is changed by a Pachakuti, a Kal is
**fixed** by one. A Kal caught in a transition does not die and does not cross. It
becomes stone.

The old mined galleries and carved halls that Kal regard as ancestral are not
buildings. They are ancestors. Every archive of Kal history is also a graveyard, and
they will not permit it to be studied as anything else.

**Mechanically:** innate structural understanding, resistance to physical damage,
slow movement, reduced spell capacity. Natural engineers, artificers, smiths,
alchemists. No affinity for magic at all.

## 3. Siann — the long view

**Aptitude: magic, science. Balance: slow, deep.**

They reach physical maturity in their late teens and then simply stop. A Siann of
twenty and a Siann of four hundred are indistinguishable at a glance, which makes
them very good at being underestimated and very bad at being trusted.

Their minds work at the pace of careers rather than moments. They learn magic slowly
and then keep going, past where anyone else stops. The oldest living things in the
setting that still have opinions are Siann.

They experience time as a resource they have in surplus, which produces a certain
detachment that other races read as arrogance, and occasionally is.

### Visual identity

The established Siann appearance remains recognizable from the existing Mira-derived
reference, but it should be pushed further away from “very pale human.”

Their skin is **opal-like and faintly translucent** rather than merely white. In thin
areas — temples, ears, fingers, throat — subtle subdermal structures may be visible as
silver-lavender traces rather than ordinary red vascularization. Light scatters softly
beneath the surface, giving healthy Siann skin an internal luminous quality without
making it literally transparent.

Their eyes are pale lavender with an unusual brighter inner ring. During magical
activity that ring may become subtly more luminous, noticeable at conversational
distance without becoming a constant glowing-eye effect.

Typical proportions include:

- a slightly elongated neck;
- long, fine hands;
- narrow shoulders;
- exceptionally controlled posture;
- facial symmetry that can feel unnervingly precise.

Avoid conventional elf shorthand. **Siann do not need pointed ears.**

Their platinum-white hair remains an age signal: the oldest Siann are the whitest, and
hair remains one of the only external clues to how long they have lived.

**Mechanically:** highest skill ceiling, slowest learning, reduced physical
resilience, no aging penalties. The long-haul caster and scholar.

*Influence note: the "immortal who regards shorter-lived people as brief" idea appears
in many traditions, Doctor Who's Time Lords among them. The mechanism here is
biological rather than technological, and nothing is drawn from that specific work —
no regeneration, no temporal Lords, no shared terminology.*

## 4. Orenth — the walkers

**Aptitude: melee, magic. Balance: mobile, high cost.**

The only race that crosses between Pachas deliberately. They do not travel through
the space between realities; they step sideways, briefly borrowing the properties of
an adjacent one to move.

This is lethal work. Each crossing costs them something — memory, years, sometimes a
sense they cannot name afterward. Older Orenth are noticeably diminished: the most
skilled walkers are also the most hollowed.

They have no homeland. No Orenth can say which Pacha their line began in, because the
knowledge is what crossing takes first.

### Visual identity

An Orenth body is broadly humanoid. The unsettling part is that reality never seems to
register it perfectly.

At rest, an Orenth may appear almost ordinary. In movement, small failures become
visible:

- edges briefly double;
- a hand appears a fraction displaced from its expected position;
- a shadow lags behind by an instant;
- a reflection resolves slightly late;
- rapid movement leaves a faint secondary silhouette.

The effect should remain subtle enough that the Orenth feel physically present, not
like permanent visual-effects clouds.

Older Orenth accumulate **phase scars**: regions of the body that no longer resolve
correctly after repeated crossings. Examples include:

- skin that seems to have almost no surface texture;
- an eyebrow that casts no shadow;
- a fingertip absent from mirrors;
- a missing segment in an iris;
- a lock of hair that becomes faintly transparent from certain angles.

Phase scars visually record the same permanent cost already present in their crossing
mechanic. They do not add a new resource or penalty by themselves.

**Mechanically:** short-range teleport and blink abilities, wraith-step, planar
anchors. Costs accumulate permanently on a hidden track — power that eats the
character. The only race that can reach places others cannot.

## 5. Mor — the stolen

**Aptitude: ethereal, magic. Balance: no body, no leverage.**

Beings native to a Pacha that no longer exists — destroyed, collapsed, or never
finished. What persists is something like a person without a body: no fixed form, no
weight, no reflection they can rely on.

They exist here only because this Pacha tolerates them. Every Mor is a guest. They
cannot be disarmed because they carry nothing, and they cannot open a door because
they have no hands. They interact with the world through will, and it is exhausting.

Necromancy is a Mor discipline in origin. Reaching across the boundary into
non-existence and pulling something back is what they are, done deliberately.

### Visual identity

Do not make the Mor conventionally physical merely to simplify production.

A Mor can maintain a person-shaped presentation because personhood is how it remembers
itself, but close inspection should reveal that the shape is an approximation:

- edges may dissolve into mist, particles or missing space;
- feet need not quite contact the floor;
- features may subtly change when attention shifts away;
- hair and clothing-like forms may behave as memory rather than fabric;
- reflections can be unreliable or absent;
- different Mor can choose very different degrees of anthropomorphism.

Their presentation should remain emotionally readable, but the audience should never
forget that the body is not actually a body.

**Mechanically:** no physical item slots, innate ethereal and spectral abilities,
excellent at necromantic and soul magic, fragile in direct combat, cannot be
disarmed, cannot carry loot conventionally.

## 6. Constructed — the written

**Aptitude: science, magic. Balance: modular, no growth.**

Sapient beings from a Pacha that was **built rather than born**. A reality engineered
into existence by a civilization that wanted one, which then kept running after its
authors were gone. Its inhabitants became people.

They are not artificial intelligences in a machine. They are natives of a synthetic
reality, which makes them simultaneously the most knowledgeable beings in the setting
about how Pacha works and the least sure they have the right to exist.

They reason in systems. A Constructed sees physics as a specification and magic as a
patch, and finds the distinction between the two faintly embarrassing.

### Visual identity

The established Constructed appearance remains: continuous liquid chrome under high
surface tension, mirror-polished and seamless, with solid glossy black eyes and an
idealized but recognizably human facial structure.

Their baseline body plan remains two-armed and two-legged.

However, their synthetic biology makes them the strongest candidate for **purposeful
modular morphology**. A sufficiently advanced Constructed may eventually install,
grow or split temporary/permanent secondary manipulator arms as a major modification.

A future four-arm configuration should have meaningful tradeoffs, potentially
including:

- increased energy/resource demand;
- greater cognitive or control load;
- reduced structural resilience;
- use of a major modification slot;
- incompatibility with some fitted equipment.

Secondary arms need not remain permanently extended. A plausible Constructed design
may allow them to merge back into the body or retract when not required, preserving
compatibility with conventional armor silhouettes.

This is **future modification design space**, not the baseline racial anatomy and not
a current implementation requirement.

**Mechanically:** strongest at magical theory, enchanting, artifice and the
manipulation of Pacha itself. Can be modified with components where other races train.
Poor at intuition, inspiration and anything that cannot be reasoned toward.

*This is the natural home for RIFT-canon material. **Decided:** RIFT characters are not
carried over, but **Glyph's race is the Constructed** — the species exists here, the
individual does not. A being from an AI-generated reality sits in this race directly,
so the archetype is available without importing continuity.*

## 7. Vaskaal — the void-farers

**Aptitude: ranged, science. Balance: fragile, unmatched technology.**

They did not come through a Pacha. They came **around** it — crossing the space that
contains Pachas, which nothing native survives. Nothing except something that was
never native.

To this world they are simply aliens, and the word fits. Their bodies should no longer
be described as wrong only in small ways: among conventional physical bipeds, the
Vaskaal are the race most immediately identifiable as having evolved elsewhere.

### Visual identity

Typical adult Vaskaal stand roughly **2.2 to 2.5 meters tall**, but are exceptionally
lean. Their torsos are narrow and comparatively shallow, with very long limbs and
little of the visual mass expected from something so tall.

Their limbs include additional articulation without simply giving them duplicate
human elbows or knees. In particular:

- the forearm contains a secondary rotational articulation;
- the ankle/foot complex has an additional flexible segment;
- shoulders allow unusually precise rotational control.

Their hands use **three long primary fingers and two opposing thumbs**. Five digits
remain useful for tool interaction, but their arrangement is unmistakably nonhuman and
supports extremely fine manipulation.

The head should avoid the conventional “grey alien” silhouette:

- no external ears;
- a narrow jaw;
- altered nasal openings rather than a strongly projecting human nose;
- deep-set or otherwise unusual reflective eyes;
- subtle throat structures that may serve heat-exchange functions.

They breathe the same usable atmosphere, but their physiology does not need to solve
heat, sound or respiration in exactly the same way.

Their gait should be recognizable at a distance: long stride, very little vertical bob,
and mechanically smooth compensation through the additional articulations.

The result is a creature that looks elegant, engineered and physically fragile rather
than powerful merely because of its height.

They are not magical. Their equipment is not enchanted, it is *better*, and the
difference matters enormously to everyone else. They are here for reasons of their
own, and the honest ones will admit the reasons are not good.

**Mechanically:** no magic at all — no mana, no spells, ever. Greatest range, best
non-magical equipment, technology that reads as incomprehensible to every other race.
Scienceless without their kit, which is the intended tension: the best-equipped and
most helpless character in the game.

Vaskaal technology is cultural/scientific knowledge, not genetic memory. Their
**biological** advantages are their morphology, senses and fine-manipulation anatomy;
their engineering still has to be learned.

## 8. Ondrek — the read-stone

**Aptitude: science, craft. Balance: composition, memory, slow.**

Thinking stone. Not Kal — or not *only* Kal, which is the whole dispute. Defined by
being the only race that reads raw material as memory, and by the fact that they are
quietly ending.

See `STONE_QUESTION.md` for the full design. In short: they are courteous, patient and
profoundly sad, they quarry Kal stone because they believe it is the only route to
continuing, and they claim to be able to tell stone that was once Kal from stone that
never was — a claim the Kal dispute because accepting it would mean accepting that
some ancestors are not in the quarries.

### Visual identity

Do not render the Ondrek as humans with a rock texture.

An Ondrek should look like **living geology that has organized itself into a mobile
person-shape**.

Their overall locomotor plan remains compatible with a head, torso, two arms and two
legs, but their external structure is naturally irregular:

- exposed strata;
- fault lines;
- mineral veins;
- crystalline inclusions;
- healed fractures containing visibly different material;
- asymmetrical plate and stone growth.

One shoulder may be basalt-heavy while the other carries crystalline growth. Age,
repair, environment and accumulated material can make older Ondrek increasingly
geologically complex.

Faces remain readable but should not simply reproduce a human face in stone.
Possibilities include:

- recessed sensory structures;
- crystalline eyes;
- movable brow plates;
- a mouth fissure rather than soft lips;
- minimal or absent human-style nose anatomy.

Their palms and fingertips contain exceptionally fine mineral structures used for
stonesense. When an Ondrek touches stone, “reading” it is not metaphorical mysticism:
their body is performing a material interaction flesh organisms cannot.

**Mechanically:** stonesense, material memory, unrivaled craft. No magic. Heavy, slow,
very hard to kill, poor at stealth, and socially disadvantaged in cultures that will
not speak to them.

## Visual references

Two races have established reference art from existing RIFT material. Use it; do not
reinvent these looks, but apply the refinements in this document.

### Constructed — from Glyph

Continuous liquid chrome under high surface tension, mirror-polished and unbroken from
crown to sole, with no seams, joints or visible fasteners. A **human, beautiful face** —
clear brow, high cheekbones, a straight narrow nose with defined nostrils, soft defined
jaw and chin — and **solid glossy black eyes** with no iris or sclera. Body smooth and
blank: no nipples, no breast detail, no navel, no genitalia.

The concept generator will not reliably render the blank chest; see the production note
in `MYTHOLOGY.md` and fix it in Blender instead. Full appearance spec and the reference
art paths are in `MYTHOLOGY.md`.

Modular morphology such as secondary arms is a later character-development option, not
a change to this baseline reference.

### Siann — from Mira

Exceptionally long straight platinum-white hair, opalescent skin a few shades paler
than any living person with a faint luminous/translucent quality, and a smooth unlined
face that reads as about eighteen with no hint of age.

Apply the race-level additions from this document:

- subtle silver-lavender subdermal structures in thin areas;
- pale lavender eyes with a brighter inner ring;
- slightly elongated neck and fine hands;
- no pointed-ear requirement.

The hair remains the age signal: the oldest Siann are the whitest, and it is the only
obvious external clue to how long they have lived. References in
`joyecho_refs\mira\` and `ComfyUI\input\mira_*`.

Both are race references, not individual characters. RIFT continuity is not carried
over — the *people* exist here, the named individuals do not.

## Body-plan, armor and animation production guidance

Race design must preserve enough shared structure that the equipment and animation
systems remain achievable.

Use a small number of **fit families** rather than one bespoke equipment system per
race. Conceptually these may eventually include families such as:

- standard humanoid;
- compact / broad humanoid;
- tall / narrow humanoid;
- irregular heavy body;
- nonphysical;
- modular synthetic.

Exact implementation names are not decided here.

Equipment does not need to magically morph onto every possible body. Crafting can
support **refitting** appropriate gear between compatible fit families. Examples:

- resizing armor panels;
- changing straps and joint clearances;
- adding Kal dorsal wing channels;
- rebuilding Vaskaal gloves and boots;
- adapting a breastplate to a mixed-lineage frame.

This creates a meaningful armorsmithing service rather than an arbitrary restriction.

The Mor remain a deliberate exception because conventional equipment is already not
part of their mechanics. Constructed modular configurations may require specialized
equipment or temporary retraction of additional structures.

## Future lineage and hybrid design

**Status: open design space, not finalized biological canon.**

Humanoid appearance does **not** automatically imply reproductive compatibility.
Several peoples may share deep ancestry and still have diverged far enough that
fertility differs dramatically.

Future character creation should define an explicit compatibility model, potentially
including:

- **naturally compatible**;
- **compatible with medical, magical or technological assistance**;
- **viable but infertile offspring** where appropriate;
- **normally incompatible**;
- **impossible without extraordinary intervention**.

The exact pairings are not decided here.

Two specific combinations raised for future investigation are:

- **Siann / Vaskaal**;
- **Kal / Veth**.

These are examples and open questions, not confirmed canon.

Mixed-lineage characters should use **trait expression**, not averaged racial stats.
A hybrid should not simply receive half of one parent's bonuses and half of the other's.
Instead, inherited morphology and innate traits should express individually, with some
features compatible, some mutually exclusive, and occasional interactions unique to
the mixed lineage.

Culture remains separate from lineage. A Siann/Vaskaal person raised entirely in
Otherhome may know less about either ancestral culture than a human adopted and trained
inside one of those societies.

## Future mutation and purposeful enhancement

**Status: future character-development space, not current implementation.**

Otherreach may eventually allow rare beneficial mutations, Other-related alterations,
biological intervention, magical modification or deliberate technological
enhancement.

Potential examples include:

- additional manipulator arms;
- a functional tail;
- enhanced or alternative vision;
- reinforced skeleton;
- altered respiration;
- sensory organs;
- Othermarked phase structures;
- specialized wings or flight adaptations.

These should follow the same principle as the wider crafting system:

> **Prefer meaningful costs and tradeoffs over arbitrary prohibition.**

An extra pair of arms might improve manipulation while increasing metabolic demand,
cognitive load and armor complexity. Enhanced night vision might introduce sensitivity
to intense light. A reinforced skeleton might increase resilience while increasing
mass and reducing swimming performance.

Such alterations should be major choices, discoveries or consequences — not a grab-bag
of free cosmetic bonuses at character creation.

## Coverage check

| Aptitude | Owning race | Secondary |
|---|---|---|
| Melee | Veth | Orenth |
| Ranged | Vaskaal | Veth |
| Magic | Siann | Mor, Orenth |
| Science | Kal | Constructed, Vaskaal, Ondrek |
| Craft | Ondrek | Kal, Constructed |
| Ethereal | Mor | Constructed |
| Reality-jumping | Orenth | — |
| Outside everything | Vaskaal | — |
| Synthetic origin | Constructed | — |

Every aptitude has one owner and one secondary, so no race is universally best and no
character is locked out of a role. Race grants an aptitude and an unusual capability,
never a prohibition — except the Vaskaal, who genuinely have no magic, and that is the
point of them.

Science has four races with a claim and craft has three. That is deliberate: an
aptitude is a leaning rather than a lock, and eight races arguing over the same
disciplines is content, not a balance problem.

Future skill design should continue to separate innate biological capability from
learnable discipline. Cultural and technical traditions belonging to one people should
generally remain teachable to outsiders when their prerequisites can be met, even when
the outsider begins at a substantial knowledge disadvantage.

## Open questions for the owner

1. **Count.** Eight is a lot to write well. Which three or four would you develop
   first for the vertical slice?
2. **The Kal stone.** Is petrified-Kal a horror, a resource, or a religious matter?
   This decides whether players can mine it, and that decides whether Kal are playable
   in good conscience.
3. **Do the Veth know?** Is the shared veth/kal/mor root a mid-game revelation or
   established scholarship the player can learn early?
4. **Vaskaal arrival.** Are they recent, and is their presence an event the player can
   witness, or old history?
5. **RIFT continuity.** Does the Constructed race carry existing RIFT characters, or
   does RIFT remain a separate continuity the Constructed merely resemble?
6. **Hybrid compatibility.** Which race pairings are naturally fertile, which require
   assistance, and which are impossible?
7. **Mixed-lineage body plans.** How much morphological variation should character
   creation permit before a hybrid requires a distinct armor fit family?
8. **Kal wings.** Is the low-gravity / dense-atmosphere ancestral explanation canon,
   and how much true powered flight remains possible in the present Pacha?
9. **Vaskaal anatomy.** Which sensory and heat-exchange structures are visible enough
   to become part of character customization without drifting into familiar grey-alien
   shorthand?
10. **Mutation and enhancement access.** Which alterations can be inherited at character
    creation, and which must only be acquired through play?
11. **Visual validation.** Before final race asset production, do all major races pass
    the solid-black silhouette test at representative gameplay distance?
