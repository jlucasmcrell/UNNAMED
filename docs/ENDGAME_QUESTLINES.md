# UNNAMED - Endgame Questlines and Access Gates

Design for the eight racial secrets as long-running endgame chains, and for the
mechanism that decides who can reach them.

Companion to `MYTHOLOGY.md` (the secrets themselves), `RACES.md` (mechanics), and
`COMPANION_MATRIX.md` (which peoples will travel with which, and what that blocks - the
constraint that decides how many of these chains one character can finish).

---

## The design problem to solve first

`MYTHOLOGY.md` gives every race a secret they will not discuss. Turning those into
endgame content runs straight into a contradiction in the project charter:

> "Everything important must be reasonably achievable by a solo player."
> "Allow failure."
> "Do not make failure constantly force save reloading."

Race-gating and companion-gating are, on their face, **permanent content denial**. A
Veth player can never be a Mor, so a Mor-gated chain would be unreachable forever, and
"reasonably achievable by a solo player" would be false.

Three ways to resolve it. The recommendation is the third.

**Option A - no gates.** Every chain open to everyone. Simplest, and it wastes the
material: a secret that anyone can read is not a secret.

**Option B - hard race gates.** Only a Mor can do the Mor chain. Strong identity, but
it is content denial, it punishes the player's race choice at character creation, and
it breaks the solo-first pillar outright.

**Option C - graduated access. Recommended.** The race gate exists but is **surmountable
at a price**, and the price is different for each tier. A dedicated player can reach any
chain; a player of that race reaches it on easy terms. This keeps race meaningful
without ever making content impossible.

---

## The access model

Four tiers of access, checked at specific moments rather than throughout. A chain is not
"gated"; particular steps ask who is standing with you.

| Tier | Requirement | Cost to the player |
|---|---|---|
| **Native** | You are that race | none — the chain is yours |
| **Adopted** | A companion of that race, present at the key steps | a party slot held for the whole chain |
| **Borrowed** | A hireling of that race, hired for the chain | gold, and hirelings can die |
| **Rented** | An NPC outside the party, retained for one step | heavy gold, reputation, or obligation |

### Presence, not possession

A companion must be **present at the gated moments**, not carried for the whole chain.
Carrying a Kal for 150 hours so they can read one inscription is busywork, and busywork
is not consequence.

Each chain therefore has **three or four gated moments** with the rest open. The player
who adopts a companion learns, somewhere through the chain, that they will be needed
again — and has to decide whether to keep them.

### What a native gets

Not a shorter chain. A native gets:

- the gated steps without a companion
- **a different final choice** — the native can settle the question from inside their
  own tradition, which an outsider can only observe
- a distinct epilogue for their people

So the race gate determines *which ending you can reach*, not whether you get one.

## The chains

Eight endgame chains, one per people. Each is written to be long — the charter's shape
is research, travel, difficult acquisition, a real choice — and each resolves the
question its race has been carrying.

---

### The Measured Dead — Kal

*Question: what is the stone?*

The chain that `STONE_QUESTION.md` describes, extended past its faction resolution into
the endgame question the Kal have refused for their entire history: whether the stone in
the quarries was ever anyone.

Runs through the Ondrek war, the Instrument, and the sealed archive. The final
revelation is a count — the number of Kal who became stone, and the number who did not.
Those two numbers cannot both be what the Kal believe, and one of them is worse.

**Gated moments:** reading the sealed archive (Kal), the Ondrek testimony (Ondrek),
the count itself (either).

---

### The Front of the Book — Siann

*Question: what did you see?*

The Siann Record is missing its first pages. The chain reconstructs them — from Vaskaal
external accounts, from Mor memory-carriers, from a Written Pacha archive that kept a
copy of something nobody knew was copied.

The revelation is not a battle. It is a **testimony**, and the player has to decide
whether to publish it. Publishing discredits the Siann as a people and is true.
Suppressing it preserves a civilisation built on accurate memory by destroying its only
honest act.

**Gated moments:** reading the archive (Siann), cross-checking against the Vaskaal
record (Vaskaal), surviving the memory-transfer (Mor).

---

### The Found and the Empty — Orenth

*Question: what was already there?*

The Found Pacha was discovered intact and empty, and the Orenth have never said what
they found. The chain reaches it — a real journey between realities, costing
permanent loss on the way.

What they found was **occupied**. Not by people. By maintenance. Something still runs
there, still performs its function, and does not regard the Orenth as either trespassers
or inhabitants. The Orenth left because they were not noticed, and have spent their
history unable to explain why that was worse than being opposed.

**Gated moments:** the crossing itself (Orenth, or a party led by one), reading the
maintenance log (Constructed), surviving at the boundary (Mor).

---

### The Author's Name — Constructed

*Question: who wrote us?*

The Written Pacha has documentation and no author. The chain traces the specification
back through its own revision history.

The answer is that the author is **in the text** — the specification was written by
something that had already specified itself, and the regress does not terminate. There
is no first author. The Constructed were not created by a mind; they are what happens
when a description describes itself for long enough.

For a Constructed player this is either liberation or annihilation, and the chain's
final choice is which the player makes it.

**Gated moments:** the revision history (Constructed), the substrate observation
(Vaskaal), the earliest layer (Mor, who can read a document that no longer exists).

---

### The Cause — the Silence

*Question: what caused the last Pachakuti?*

The only chain that belongs to no race, and the one all eight converge on. Every
people's account is a fragment, and the full picture requires access to all of them —
which is to say, it requires having done most of the other seven.

This is the intended final chain, and it should be reachable only late. Its answer is
the setting's spine and should be built last, once the others establish what each
partial account claims.

**Gated moments:** one per race, at minimum. Deliberately the hardest chain in the game.

---

### The Weather — Vaskaal

*Question: what is in the substrate?*

The Vaskaal crossed the space between Pachas and will not say what else is in it. The
chain establishes that they did not cross it; they were **carried**, and the thing that
carried them is still holding them. The expedition cannot leave because leaving is not
something they are permitted.

This reframes the Vaskaal from explorers to cargo, and their internal politics from a
dispute into a symptom.

**Gated moments:** the ship (Vaskaal), reading the substrate readings (Constructed),
being noticed (Mor).

---

### The Accusation — Mor

*Question: who killed your world?*

The Mor believe their Pacha was deliberately destroyed and want a culprit. The chain
finds one — with evidence, names, and a motive — and the evidence is good.

The turn: the culprit was **Mor**. The Lost Pacha was destroyed from inside, by its own
people, in a war the survivors have collectively forgotten because destroying the
record was part of the destruction. Every Mor who has spent centuries hunting the
answer is hunting themselves.

The chain's choice is whether to tell them.

**Gated moments:** the forensic reconstruction (Mor), the surviving witnesses
(Siann — the only other people who were there), the final confirmation (Constructed).

---

### The Ending — Ondrek

*Question: can a people that is ending choose how?*

The Ondrek are dying and the Stone Question chain settles their dispute with the Kal.
This chain is what comes after: whether they continue by some other means, accept the
end, or become something that is no longer Ondrek.

It is the only chain with no villain and no revelation — only a decision, made slowly,
by a people who have had centuries to think about it.

**Gated moments:** the emergence site (Ondrek), the reading method (Kal), the
alternative (Constructed).

## Companion consequences

The gates make companions structurally load-bearing, which is the point. But it
creates obligations the design has to meet:

**Companions must not be missable in a way that locks a chain.** Every race must have
at least one recruitable companion available through ordinary play — so a player who
wants the Kal chain can always find a Kal. Losing one to a story decision should
provide another route, or the chain should record the loss and change.

**Hirelings are the fallback, and they die.** This is where "borrowed" access earns its
tension: a player who will not commit a party slot can rent the access, and a rented
witness can be killed on the way. The chain survives; the cost does not.

**A companion carried to the end should be changed by it.** The charter asks for
companions with loyalties, rivalries, fears and personal quests. A Kal companion who
watches you publish the count is not the same character afterwards, and that should be
mechanical, not flavour text.

**No companion should be a key.** If a companion is only ever a lock to be turned, they
are an equipment slot with a face — the exact failure the charter names. Each gated
moment should reveal something about the companion that the player did not know, so the
party member is a person in the scene rather than a pass.

## Open questions

1. **How many chains can one character finish?** If all eight are reachable with
   effort, a completionist run is enormous. If only some are, the player's choice of
   which secrets to pursue becomes the real endgame decision — which is likely better.
2. **Should the Silence chain require all eight, or any four?** All eight is a
   completionist gate. Any four is a choice.
3. **Do companions leave?** A Kal who objects to the outcome should be able to walk,
   and that should be permanent.
4. **Are native endings better?** They must not be — a Veth who adopts a Kal companion
   and sees it through has earned the same revelation, if not the same voice.
