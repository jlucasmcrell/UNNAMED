# OTHERREACH — Quests, Dungeons, World Events & Repopulation

**Status:** Strong working design  
**M2 impact:** None

## Core rule

> **Content happens in the world; it does not always wait frozen for the player.**

Important authored content should usually transform into another opportunity rather than vanish pointlessly.

## Timed quests

Use deadlines only when fiction creates one:

- ransom;
- spoilage;
- inauguration;
- caravan departure;
- dying patient;
- eclipse/ritual;
- scheduled meeting.

Avoid arbitrary countdowns on ordinary exploration.

## Deadline consequences

Failure can change the situation:

- hostage moved/killed/escaped;
- ceremony proceeds;
- ingredient spoils;
- caravan leaves;
- target relocates;
- political state changes.

Prefer consequence over a simple `QUEST FAILED`.

## NPC completion

Autonomous adventurers may complete systemic and competitive opportunities.

They evaluate:

- difficulty;
- reward;
- travel;
- timing;
- role composition;
- risk.

## Notifications and discovery

Diegetic/configurable channels can report interesting outcomes:

- guild board;
- town crier;
- newspaper;
- rumor;
- letter;
- faction intelligence.

A resolved opportunity can link to where that type of quest originates so missing content teaches the player about the world.

## NPC progression

NPCs improve at plausible rates through time, activity, training, equipment, and success/failure.

## LFG and expedition boards

NPC parties can recruit for roles:

- healer;
- warding;
- ranged;
- lock/trap;
- scout;
- tracker.

The player can join, ignore, or lose the slot to another NPC.

Ambient LFG chatter should generally use cheap structured text rather than constant runtime LLM inference.

## Dungeons as persistent places

A dungeon may be:

- occupied;
- contested;
- cleared;
- vacant;
- claimed;
- sealed;
- collapsed;
- repurposed.

## Repopulation

Avoid identical timer respawns where possible.

New occupants can arrive through:

- migration;
- faction expansion;
- bandits;
- animals;
- refugees;
- smugglers;
- cults;
- undead sources;
- Other incursions.

A cleared crypt may later become a smuggler hideout, refugee shelter, player home, NPC base, cult site, or monster nest.

## Claiming locations

Where law and physics allow, a cleared location can become a home, stash, faction base, or settlement outpost.

## Physical world mutation

Locations can change through:

- roof collapse;
- giant-creature breach;
- flood;
- cave-in;
- fire;
- new gate;
- construction.

Persistent overlays should alter authored locations without requiring full replacement.

## Bosses

Do not assume identical boss respawn.

### Unique mortal
Dead stays dead.

### Role replacement
A new leader replaces the old one.

### Biological replacement
Another creature eventually claims territory.

### Supernatural return
Only when lore supports it.

## Loot

Unique artifacts stay unique unless lore says otherwise.

A later occupant has different equipment/loot.

## Content classes

- **Personal story** — primarily tied to the player's narrative.
- **Systemic opportunity** — any eligible actor may complete.
- **Competitive world opportunity** — first actor/party wins.
- **Shared world event** — many actors contribute.
- **Persistent epic** — large authored arc advances over time while preserving participation paths.

## Hidden epic starts

Epic arcs need not advertise themselves.

Examples:

- rescue an old wizard's dog;
- shelter an escaped Constructed;
- return an odd object;
- help an unknown traveler.

A small humane/curious action can reveal a huge arc later.

## Dormant story seeds

The world can contain dormant seeds activated through interaction. Some may be player-focused; others may be discovered by NPCs.

## World Event Director

Use a systemic scheduler with controlled randomness considering:

- world age;
- current events;
- population;
- factions;
- recent event intensity;
- geographic/cosmic conditions;
- dormant seeds.

Events should feel unexpected while remaining paced and internally legal.

## Event scale

- Personal
- Local
- Regional
- Continental
- Cosmological

## Cosmic example

A moon cracks open and reveals a planet-scale dragon or organism.

Consequences may include changes to sky, religion, markets, migration, magic, Othertides, Otherways, governments, and animal behavior.

This is not simply a giant HP bar. The response may require research, diplomacy, artifacts, ritual, Great Works, Otherway manipulation, or survival.

## Town destruction and reconstruction

Possible settlement progression:

**prosperous → threatened → besieged → occupied → ruined**

Then perhaps:

**ruined → refugee camp → rebuilding → restored/changed**

Rebuilding needs real resources, labor, specialists, security, and money.

NPCs may rebuild without the player.

## Hostile settlements

Enemy towns should still have food, trade, craftsmen, housing, guards, families/inhabitants where appropriate, and ideology/politics.

Conquest should create economic/population consequences.

## Reclaiming cities

A major arc may require:

- removing occupiers;
- securing infrastructure;
- returning refugees;
- restoring defenses;
- rebuilding markets;
- choosing political control.

## History journal

Resolved opportunities can remain as historical records:

- issuer;
- winner;
- outcome;
- world change;
- how the player learned of it.

## Design rule

> **The world may resolve content without the player, but important authored content should usually change form rather than simply disappear.**
