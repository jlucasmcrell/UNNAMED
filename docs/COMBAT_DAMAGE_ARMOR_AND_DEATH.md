# OTHERREACH — Combat, Damage, Armor, Weapons & Death

**Status:** Foundational design direction  
**M2 impact:** None  
**Future schema impact:** Significant; reconcile after M2.

## 1. Combat philosophy

> **Weapons, armor, anatomy, positioning, knowledge and condition determine the result. Level influences competence, but does not replace physical logic.**

A powerful character should be hard to kill because they are skilled, prepared, equipped, protected, altered or genuinely superhuman — not because a number beside their name makes a knife through the brain harmless.

## 2. Combat feel

Target:

**first-person action combat with meaningful timing and positioning, without becoming a mandatory hardcore medieval-combat simulator.**

Player skill and character skill both matter.

## 3. Weapon capability model

Weapons should expose capabilities rather than only a class label:

- reach;
- mass;
- balance;
- grip;
- cut;
- thrust;
- impact;
- penetration;
- speed/recovery;
- concealability;
- noise;
- hand requirement;
- projectile behavior;
- mechanisms;
- magical/technical channels.

## 6. Race/culture weapon identity

### Kal
Compact dense weapons, picks, counterweighted hammers, structural tools, possible wing-assisted momentum designs.

### Vaskaal
Precision technology, recoil-compensated projectiles, flechettes, smart sights, tools designed for unusual hands.

### Siann
Precise focus weapons, elegant multi-mode forms, conductor blades/staves.

### Orenth
Anchoring/phase/displacement weapons.

### Ondrek
Resonance/fracture tools using material knowledge.

### Constructed
Reconfigurable modular implements.

### Mor
Spectral/soul-contact techniques rather than conventional carried weapons.

## 9. Magic implements

Different implements change expression, not merely spell-power numbers.

### Wand
Fast, precise, one-handed.

### Staff
Stable, powerful, two-handed, viable physical weapon.

### Rod
Compact, often charge-oriented.

### Scepter
Control/authority/command focus.

### Orb
Sustained field/divination/control.

### Grimoire
Preparation/ritual/complexity.

### Talisman/amulet/ring
Passive/conditional/low-capacity convenience.

### Athame/ritual blade
Close-combat ritual intersection.

### Runestone
Portable pattern/anchor.

### Censer/bell/instrument
Area/resonance/spirit traditions.

### Lens
Precision/perception/focusing.

### Gauntlet
Combat casting integrated with hand movement.

### Body inscription
Hard to disarm, hard to change, potentially costly.

Do not assume universal mana; implements may use different resource channels.

## 10. Hybrid implements

Candidate setting-specific pieces:

- **Veil collar** — resists soul/phase effects;

- **Phase laminate** — defense against displacement/phase attacks.

These names are working vocabulary, not yet canon.

## 15. Armor interaction

Three broad solutions to armor:

### Overcome
Impact through/against it.

### Penetrate
Pierce the protection.

### Bypass
Target a gap/uncovered anatomy.

## 16. Shields

Shields are physically positioned equipment.

Examples:
- buckler;
- round;
- heater;
- kite;
- large infantry;
- pavise/tower;
- magical barrier focus.

Coverage, speed, mass and stamina differ.

## 17. Locational damage

Do not simulate every finger as separate HP.

Useful regions:

- head;
- torso;
- arms;
- legs;
- species-specific vital structures.

Consequences may include:

- impaired movement;
- poorer weapon handling;
- concentration/focus loss;
- bleeding;
- stagger.

## 18. Hit resolution

Conceptually:

**attack → contact location → armor layers → penetration/impact → anatomy → wound/stagger/health outcome**

## 19. Vital/weak-point lethality

If an appropriate weapon reaches an unprotected critical structure, the result can be lethal regardless of level.

Examples:

- brain penetration;
- major neck vessel;
- species-specific organ;
- technological power core.

Higher-level characters are harder targets because of awareness, movement, armor, wards, skill and preparation.

## 20. Called shots and anatomy knowledge

Possible broad targets:

- head;
- neck;
- torso;
- arms;
- legs.

Advanced knowledge can reveal:

- eye;
- artery;
- joint gap;
- heart/lung;
- gland;
- magical organ;
- power system;
- wing joint.

Knowledge can come from anatomy, hunting, medicine, bestiary study and cultural knowledge.

## 21. Grappling

Support a simplified but meaningful close-control system:

- grab;
- shove;
- clinch;
- trip;
- pin;
- disarm;
- weapon control;
- choke/garrote where anatomy permits.

Reach weapons should become awkward at extreme close range.

## 22. Fundamental combat resources

Working baseline:

- Health;
- Stamina;
- possibly Focus/Posture/Stability depending on final combat prototype.

Do not lock exact bars until prototype testing.

## 23. Damage and status families

Physical:
- cut/slash;
- pierce;
- blunt/impact.

Other effects only when behavior differs meaningfully:
- heat/fire;
- cold;
- electrical;
- poison;
- disease;
- bleed;
- necromantic/soul;
- Other/phase;
- technological/energy;
- concussive.

Avoid dozens of arbitrary resistance percentages.

## 24. Injury states

Suggested broad life states:

**Healthy → Wounded → Critical → Downed/Incapacitated → Dying → Dead**

Zero combat effectiveness need not instantly equal death.

## 25. Downed

Possible behaviors:
- crawl;
- surrender;
- call for help;
- receive first aid;
- be captured/rescued.

## 26. Dying

Represents genuine life-threatening processes:

- major blood loss;
- organ destruction;
- drowning;
- catastrophic poison;
- severe trauma.

Rapid intervention matters.

## 27. First aid, medicine and healing

### First aid
- stop bleeding;
- splint;
- stabilize;
- clean/protect.

### Medicine
- diagnose;
- surgery;
- poison/disease treatment;
- long recovery.

### Healing magic
Can exceed mundane limits but should have its own knowledge/cost/limitations.

Do not make every healer simply refill an HP bar.

## 28. Defeat outcomes

Context matters.

### Bandits
Rob/capture/ransom.

### Guards
Arrest.

### Animals
Flee, drag, defend territory, feed, or kill depending on behavior.

### Intelligent enemies
Capture/interrogate/sacrifice where appropriate.

### Companions/friendly adventurers
Rescue/stabilize.

## 29. Surrender

Allow surrender where the opponent can understand and accept it.

Enemy personality, faction and reputation influence outcome.

## 30. Death and resurrection

Death should matter.

If resurrection exists, it should be a separate supernatural process potentially requiring:

- intact/recoverable body;
- soul continuity;
- time;
- specialist;
- ritual;
- infrastructure;
- rare materials.

Possible costs:
- memory;
- Othermarking;
- scars;
- temporary impairment;
- long recovery;
- increasing instability.

Exact resurrection canon remains open.

## 31. Race-specific death

Later define how death works for:

- Mor coherence;
- Constructed repair/personhood;
- Ondrek structural loss;
- Kal dense anatomy;
- Orenth crossing;
- Vaskaal medicine.

## 32. Level rule

> **Character level never grants categorical immunity to physically plausible attacks.**

A low-level character with enough weapon, knowledge, surprise and accuracy may kill something stronger. The stronger target should usually be difficult for concrete reasons.

## 33. Artifacts

Artifacts may break established rules deliberately.

They feel extraordinary because ordinary gear obeys the rules.
