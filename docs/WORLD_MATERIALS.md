# UNNAMED — World Materials, Flora and Reagents

Catalogue of harvestable and craftable substances, plus the surface textures a world
needs. Sits alongside the two cosmologies rather than competing with them.

Companion to `OTHERREACH_COSMOLOGY.md` (the Other, the Veil, Otherwrought goods),
`MYTHOLOGY.md` (Pacha, the eight peoples), and `WORLD_BUILDING_AND_PROPERTY_DESIGN.md`
(regions, procedural dressing, resource areas).

---

## How the two cosmologies relate here

The setting has **two descriptions of the same underlying strangeness**, and no culture
holds the complete account. That is a feature, not a loose end, and it organises the
material list:

- **Pacha** is the scholarly, structural account — realities as discrete things with
  transitions between them. Its vocabulary is used by the Siann, the Constructed and the
  Orenth.
- **The Other** is the experiential, folk and religious account — a boundary, a Veil, and
  routes through it. Its vocabulary is used by everyone else, including people who have
  never heard of a Pacha.

So a Siann archivist writes *Pachakuti* in a ledger while the farmer outside calls the
same event *the Veil thinning*. The materials below are deliberately named from **both**
traditions, because a world where everyone uses the same words for the same thing is a
world that has already solved its own mystery.

## 1. Ores and metals

Ordinary industry first, so the extraordinary has something to be extraordinary *against*.

| Material | Where | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bog iron** | marsh, lowland | basic tools, nails | Common, poor quality, everyone has it |
| **Grey iron** | hills, all regions | weapons, structural | The default metal |
| **Deep iron** | mine shafts below 40 m | good steel | Needs real mining infrastructure |
| **Charcoal steel** | produced, not found | weapons, armour | The Kal make the best and will not say how |
| **Copper** | volcanic, desert | coinage, wire, alloys | Soft; used for currency and conduction |
| **Tin** | river gravels | bronze | Placer deposits, easy to pan |
| **Bronze** | produced | bells, statues, early weapons | The material of the older civilizations |
| **Lead** | deep veins | seals, weights, containment | Also the traditional liner for containing things that should stay contained |
| **Silver** | mountain, cold regions | coinage, warding | Culturally the metal of oaths and boundaries |
| **Electrum** | rare, silver-gold mix | high-status objects | Naturally occurring; Veth magistrates wear it |
| **Gold** | deep, river placer | coinage, ornament | Soft, ornamental, politically loaded |
| **Vaskaal alloy** | not mined — salvaged | their equipment only | Cannot be reproduced; a plot resource, not a craft one |

### Other-touched metals

| Material | Description | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Othersteel** | An alloy that incorporates Other-derived material. Slightly wrong in reflected light; the reflection lags a fraction behind the movement. | High-tier weapons and armour. `Otherwrought`. |
| **Veiliron** | Iron smelted in a place where the Veil is thin. Takes an edge that holds against things that are not entirely here. | Anti-ethereal weapons; the Mor cannot hold it. |
| **Coldbloom** | Metal that grew rather than was worked — found, not made, in thin-Veil sites. Fractal, faintly organic in structure. | Orenth crossing anchors. Nobody can smelt more. |

## 2. Stone

| Material | Where | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fieldstone** | everywhere | walls, foundations | The default. |
| **Grey slate** | river valleys | roofing, flooring | Veth settlements. |
| **Limestone** | lowland, karst | mortar, building, lime | Burns to lime; the bones of ordinary construction. |
| **Granite** | mountain, upland | monuments, Ker holds | The Kal build in granite and will not quarry worked stone. |
| **Basalt** | volcanic | roads, mills | Wears slowly; the Ondrek favour it. |
| **Marble** | rare, ancient seabeds | temples, statuary | Status material; Siann archives. |
| **Soapstone** | mountain, near hot springs | carving, vessels | Soft; the Ondrek can read it easily. |
| **Flint** | chalk, river gravel | fire, early tools | Still used by rural peoples and for firesteels. |
| **Obsidian** | volcanic glass | blades, mirrors | The traditional material for **scrying mirrors** — a folk Othergate. |
| **Chalk** | lowland | marking, whitewash, lime | Also used to draw protective circles. |

### Other-touched stone

| Material | Description | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Otherstone** | Found near dimensional disturbance, or brought from elsewhere. Warm to the touch, slightly too heavy, and always a little damp. | Gates, anchors, architecture, magical storage, settlement defences. |
| **Kal stone** | Petrified Kal. **Do not treat as a resource.** This is the entire subject of `STONE_QUESTION.md`. | Sacred, disputed, and the moral centre of the setting. Any "gathering" of it is a war crime to one people and a necessity to another. |
| **Veinrock** | Stone with visible Otherglass inclusions. Beautiful and slightly nauseating to look at directly. | Ornament, portal frames, expensive folly. |

| Stone | Appearance | Use |

## 6. Cosmic and Other-derived ingredients

The top of the crafting ladder, where the two cosmologies visibly disagree.

| Material | Description | Use | Rarity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Otherfire** | Flame from contact between realities. Burns without fuel, in colours that do not appear in ordinary fire, and does not consume what it burns. | Powering devices, revealing folds, damaging magical structures. | Extraordinary |
| **Veilwater** | Condensate collected where the Veil is thin. Tastes of a place you have not been. | Solvent for every Other-reagent | Very rare |
| **Pachakuti residue** | Physical deposit left at a transition site. Grey, fine, and nobody agrees whether it is a mineral. | The only material that reliably stabilises a fold | Site-specific |
| **Otherwake traces** | The residue a crossing leaves, like a ship's wake. | Trackable; used in detection and pursuit | Common near routes, worthless away from them |
| **Silence shard** | A fragment of somewhere that no longer exists. Casts no shadow and makes no sound when struck. | The most powerful and least understood reagent | Legendary |
| **Firstfold iron** | Metal from the oldest Othergates. Pre-dates every known civilization. | Cannot be worked by any current people; only re-shaped | Singular |
| **Nullstone** | Absorbs magic absolutely. Heavy, cold, and universally unpopular. | Anti-magic containment; prison construction | Rare and regulated |
| **Tideglass** | Glass formed during an Othertide, from sand that was briefly somewhere else. | Lenses that see across distances, not just magnify | Tide-dependent |

## 7. Flora for world dressing

Procedural dressing needs a vocabulary of plants that belong to *this* world rather than
to generic fantasy. Grouped by region so a biome can be assembled from a list.

### Temperate / Veth heartland
Oak, ash, elm, hazel, willow, blackthorn, hawthorn, bramble, dogrose, foxglove, comfrey,
nettle, meadowsweet, wood sorrel, bluebell, bracken, honeysuckle, ivy, mosses, bracket
fungi, field mushroom.

### Boreal / cold
Pine, spruce, larch, birch, juniper, rowan, aspen, ling heather, cloudberry, bilberry,
reindeer moss, fly agaric, chanterelle, bearberry, dwarf willow, cotton grass.

### Wetland / marsh
Reed, rush, sedge, willow, alder, bog myrtle, sundew, bladderwort, sphagnum, marsh
marigold, water lily, purple loosestrife, kingcup, horsetail.

### Steppe / dry
Feather grass, fescue, sagebrush, thyme, lavender, wormwood, soapwort, sea holly,
eryngo, thistle, silverweed, liquorice.

### Volcanic / ashen
Fireweed, ash willow, lichen crusts, sulphur moss, emberthorn, cinder grass, obsidian
flake fungus, smokeweed.

### Otherworld flora — where flora is *wrong*
These are the plants of thin-Veil sites and the Other itself. They should read as
plausible and slightly incorrect: recognisable in outline, wrong in detail.

- **Mirrorfern** — fronds that reflect rather than transpire. Cool to the touch.
- **Listening grass** — bends toward sound rather than light.
- **Boneleaf** — white, skeletal, grows on Otherstone.
- **Slowtree** — a tree that moves, visibly, over days. Which way depends on who is asked.
- **Recursive bramble** — thorns that appear to grow in a pattern that hurts to examine.
- **Hollow canopy** — a tree whose crown is a gap rather than a mass.

## 9. Asset id convention

Existing convention is `<category>_<subject>[_<variant>]`. For this catalogue:

```
item_<material>.png           ore, ingot, gem, refined material
resource_<material>.png       raw harvestable node
herb_<name>.png               plant, picked or growing
reagent_<source>.png          creature-derived
texture_<group>_<name>.png    tiling surface set
```

## 10. Open questions

1. **Does Otherstone obey the Pacha model?** If Otherstone is material from another Pacha, the Siann would classify it; if it is from outside all Pachas, they cannot. This decides whether the two cosmologies contradict or nest.
2. **How rare is Otherglass?** Rarity drives whether it is a crafting tier or a plot object.
3. **Is Tidepearl renewable?** If pearl oysters survive tides, it is an economy; if not, it is a treasure.
4. **Do the eight peoples each have a signature material?** The Kal have stone, the Ondrek read it, the Vaskaal have their alloy. The others are unassigned.
5. **Should reagents come from the existing creatures only**, or are new harvestable creatures needed? Current list sources from assets already built, which is cheaper.
