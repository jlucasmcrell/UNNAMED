# OTHERREACH — Stealth, Detection, Threat & Information Propagation

**Status:** Foundational design direction  
**M2 impact:** None

## 1. Sacred rule

> **Enemies react to what they can plausibly perceive, infer or communicate — not to what the engine knows happened.**

No universal encounter telepathy.

## 2. Perception channels

Actors may perceive through:

### Vision
- line of sight;
- lighting;
- distance;
- movement;
- camouflage;
- peripheral vision.

### Sound
- footsteps;
- armor;
- bow release;
- impact;
- shouting;
- combat;
- breaking objects;
- spell/gun discharge.

### Scent
Especially relevant to animals/monsters:
- blood;
- wind;
- species;
- masking scents.

### Tracks/signs
- footprints;
- broken foliage;
- doors;
- bodies;
- blood;
- displaced objects.

### Communication
- speech/shouting;
- horns;
- bells;
- magical alarm;
- radio/technology;
- hive/chemical signals.

### Special senses
Only when fiction supports them:
- soul detection;
- thermal;
- motion;
- vibration;
- Other disturbance.

## 3. Awareness is not binary

Suggested states:

**Unaware → Suspicious → Investigating → Alerted → Searching → Uneasy → Unaware**

Alerted does **not** mean omniscient.

## 4. Last known position

Once visual contact is lost, an actor knows:

- last seen location;
- direction;
- elapsed time;
- possibly description.

It does not receive live player coordinates.

## 5. Pulling and kiting

Old-school tactical pulling should work.

If Enemy A notices the player but Enemy B has no plausible sensory or communication path, B remains unaware.

B may join when:

- A shouts;
- B sees pursuit;
- combat becomes audible;
- alarm is triggered;
- faction communication reaches them.

## 6. Social behavior is not psychic aggro

Same faction does not imply instant shared awareness.

A fortified military base can be difficult because it deliberately has:

- overlapping sightlines;
- patrols;
- whistles;
- bells;
- magical alarms;
- radios.

A loose bandit camp may be much easier to isolate.

## 7. Noise propagation

Do not reduce every sound to a single flat radius.

Factors can include:

- source volume;
- walls;
- doors;
- terrain;
- vegetation;
- weather;
- wind;
- ambient noise;
- underground reverberation.

Approximation is acceptable; plausibility is the target.

## 8. Bodies and evidence

A silent kill does not alert unseen NPCs.

A later patrol discovering the body may:

- inspect;
- raise alarm;
- search;
- change routes;
- lock entrances;
- communicate description.

Body hiding can therefore matter.

## 9. Information records

Conceptually an NPC may know:

- hostile suspected?;
- identity/description;
- last known position;
- time observed;
- weapon seen;
- confidence;
- source of information.

Information can become stale.

## 10. Information propagation

When Guard A tells Guard B “archer near north wall,” B learns that report — not the player's exact current position.

This same system later supports:

- crime witnesses;
- rumors;
- faction alerts;
- disguises.

## 11. Disguise and recognition

Recognition can depend on:

- face seen;
- clothing;
- race/body silhouette;
- voice;
- distinctive equipment;
- reputation;
- previous encounters.

Changing clothing helps only when identity was not firmly established.

## 12. True stealth lethality

Stealth should create favorable physical conditions rather than rely only on giant damage multipliers.

An unaware target:

- is not dodging;
- is not blocking;
- may expose armor gaps;
- allows careful aim;
- may be stationary.

A dagger through an unprotected vital area or an appropriate headshot can kill.

## 13. Level interaction

A low-level attacker can potentially kill a stronger humanoid with:

- enough penetration;
- correct weak point;
- good weapon;
- surprise;
- precise execution.

The stronger target's advantages are concrete:

- awareness;
- armor;
- movement;
- better senses;
- wards;
- training;
- anatomy;
- equipment.

## 14. Bosses

Do not simply set `stealth_immune`.

A difficult boss can resist assassination through:

- armor;
- redundant anatomy;
- vibration/scent;
- wards;
- sleeping behavior;
- thick skull;
- protected eyes;
- unusual organs.

Rare discovered weaknesses may still enable unusually early victories.

## 15. Stealth disciplines

Potential related skills/knowledge:

- stealth;
- awareness;
- tracking;
- hunting;
- marksmanship;
- anatomy;
- disguise;
- infiltration/security.

A woodland hunter and an urban infiltrator should not be identical builds.

## 16. Enemy self-preservation

Alerted enemies may:

- hide;
- retreat;
- call help;
- raise shields;
- flank;
- suppress likely positions;
- wait;
- flee.

Do not force every enemy to sprint directly at an unseen shooter.

## 17. Morale and intelligence

Behavior varies by:

- intelligence;
- training;
- morale;
- species;
- faction;
- personality;
- wounds.

## 18. Animals and scent

Pets/animals create asymmetric stealth.

A guard dog can detect scent through visual concealment.

Weather/wind/masking can create counters.

## 19. Magic/technology detection

No universal “reveal hidden enemies” unless a specific extraordinary effect truly does that.

Detection types should have blind spots and counters.

## 20. Design rule

> Stealth is successful when the character prevents or manipulates information, not when a hidden meter says “invisible.”
