# OTHERREACH — Crime, Law, Reputation & Justice

**Status:** Strong working design  
**M2 impact:** None

## Foundational rule

> **What happened, what was perceived, what people believe happened, what can be proven, and what the law says about it are different things.**

The simulation records objective history. The justice system does not automatically know it.

## Objective event vs legal interpretation

The world can record who acted, who was harmed, where, when, what property changed hands, and what evidence resulted. Jurisdictions then interpret those facts.

A killing might legally become murder, manslaughter, self-defense, lawful bounty action, execution, duel, wartime action, or an unresolved death.

## Jurisdiction

Law belongs to places and institutions.

A jurisdiction can regulate:

- ownership and theft;
- weapons;
- hunting/poaching;
- necromancy;
- summoning and mind magic;
- grave excavation;
- Kal ancestral stone;
- Vaskaal technology;
- Othergate use;
- building/trespass;
- bounty authority;
- punishment.

The same act can be legal in one place and severe crime in another.

## Witnesses

Witnesses perceive events, not game-state labels.

They may know:

- rough body/race description;
- clothing;
- weapon;
- face visibility;
- direction of travel;
- time;
- distance;
- lighting;
- confidence.

Witnesses can be sincerely wrong because of darkness, fear, distance, intoxication, injury, unfamiliar anatomy, distraction, or elapsed time.

Witnesses can also deliberately lie because of loyalty, fear, greed, blackmail, revenge, faction pressure, or criminal ties.

## Guards and corruption

Guards have personalities and values such as honor, greed, duty, fear, ambition, prejudice, and loyalty.

One guard may accept a bribe. Another may arrest the player for offering it. Another may accept and later blackmail the player.

## Evidence

Potential evidence includes:

- blood;
- bodies;
- weapons;
- footprints/tracks;
- damaged locks;
- stolen property;
- magical residue;
- spell signatures;
- Otherwake;
- technological records;
- documents;
- testimony;
- timing/location evidence.

Evidence should have provenance and confidence, enabling forged, planted, contradictory, destroyed, or weak evidence.

## Framing

An NPC with motive and access can plant goods, forge correspondence, bribe/intimidate witnesses, or exploit the player's criminal associations.

Criminal association raises suspicion rather than automatically proving guilt.

## Identity and disguise

Authorities may know:

**unknown attacker → body/race description → clothing/equipment → face → name → confirmed legal identity**

Disguise is strongest before identity is firm.

Useful changes can include clothing, cloak, armor, mask/helmet, hairstyle, facial hair, cosmetics, or conspicuous equipment.

Laying low can work through leaving the jurisdiction, changing appearance, avoiding known associates, and reducing exposure.

## Information travel

Reports and warrants propagate through real communication:

- couriers;
- pigeons;
- caravans;
- magical messages;
- Vaskaal communication;
- Othergate networks.

Remote places do not learn about crimes instantly unless infrastructure permits it.

## Wanted-state layers

Potential states:

- suspicious;
- person of interest;
- identified suspect;
- wanted;
- fugitive;
- notorious;
- infamous.

Do not collapse them into one bounty number.

## Crime families

- Property: theft, burglary, trespass, vandalism, fencing, fraud.
- Violence: assault, attempted murder, manslaughter, murder.
- Public order: unlawful dueling, restricted weapons, disorder.
- Economic: smuggling, counterfeiting, contract fraud.
- Cultural/religious: grave desecration, forbidden ritual, ancestral-stone violation.
- Political: treason, sabotage, espionage.
- Travel/technology: illegal gate use, border violations, restricted technology.

## Self-defense

The system should preserve who initiated aggression, prior threats, witnesses, injuries, and force used. Jurisdictions may interpret proportionality differently.

## Bounty hunting

Bounty hunting can be a licensed profession.

Possible contracts:

- capture alive;
- capture preferred / lethal authorized;
- dead or alive;
- confirmed kill;
- recover property;
- repossession;
- locate fugitive;
- escort prisoner.

Proof can vary by jurisdiction and technology.

## Repossession

Repo work can recover leased equipment, wagons, secured trade goods, stolen Vaskaal gear, or bonded artifacts. It creates legal/economic stories without requiring murder.

## Nonlethal capture and surrender

Tools can include nets, mancatchers, restraints, bolas, clubs, grappling, sedatives, binding magic, and stun technology.

NPCs and players can surrender. Reaction depends on personality, faction, reputation, and orders.

## Arrest

Typical flow:

**demand surrender → player choice → restraint/processing**

Choices include surrender, negotiate, bribe, flee, or resist. Resistance adds consequences.

## Sentencing

Possible penalties:

- warning;
- restitution;
- fine;
- confiscation;
- license loss;
- service/labor;
- imprisonment;
- exile;
- execution.

Severity depends on offense, intent, victim, prior record, cooperation, restitution, jurisdiction, magistrate traits, and corruption.

## Prison

Prison hurts because **world time passes without the character**.

Consequences can include:

- unpaid rent/eviction;
- closed business;
- expired contracts;
- neglected crops;
- companion decisions;
- attacks on remote property;
- market changes;
- seasons/weather;
- NPC adventurer progression.

Use a short illustrated/rendered montage with dates, letters, world-news snippets, and release.

Prison can also create contacts, enemies, rumors, underworld relationships, and teachers.

## Reputation

Avoid one morality meter.

Conceptual layers:

- individual relationship;
- settlement reputation;
- faction standing;
- cultural standing;
- professional reputation;
- underworld reputation;
- criminal notoriety;
- actual legal status/warrants.

Legal acquittal does not guarantee social trust.

## Criminal organizations

A thieves' network can contain fences, burglars, smugglers, forgers, safe houses, informants, corrupt officials, contraband markets, and criminal contracts.

Criminal groups can enforce their own rules.

## NPC crime

NPCs can steal, smuggle, assault, flee, become wanted, be framed, serve prison time, or collect bounties.

## Player settlements

Later, player-influenced settlements may set policies for magic, weapons, extradition, property, bounty hunting, punishment, taxation, and guard funding.

## Design rule

> **The justice system does not know the truth. The simulation does.**
