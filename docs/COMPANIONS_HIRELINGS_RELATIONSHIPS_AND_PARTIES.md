# OTHERREACH — Companions, Hirelings, Relationships & Parties

**Status:** Strong working design  
**M2 impact:** None

## Core distinction

> **A companion is a person who chooses to travel with you. A hireling performs a job under contract. A temporary ally cooperates because immediate goals align.**

Characters can move between these states over time.

## Normal party size

Working target:

> **Player + up to 3 active companions/hirelings**

Larger groups remain possible for raids, caravans, settlement defense, rescue missions, and major expeditions.

## Companion identity

A companion has:

- goals;
- beliefs;
- relationships;
- money;
- skills;
- equipment;
- property/home;
- factions;
- fears;
- personal quests;
- moral limits.

They do not cease existing when dismissed.

## Relationships

Avoid one approval score.

Useful dimensions:

- affection;
- trust;
- respect;
- loyalty;
- fear;
- attraction;
- ideological agreement.

Someone can like the player, trust them, respect their skill, and still despise their necromancy.

## Values

Companions react according to personality/culture, not universal Good/Evil.

They can comply, hesitate, refuse, argue, leave, intervene, or report the player.

## Hirelings

Hirelings care about:

- wages;
- contract duration;
- risk;
- legality;
- supplies;
- treatment;
- expected loot.

Nonpayment or abuse can end the relationship.

## Equipment ownership

Distinguish:

- personal equipment;
- player-issued/loaned equipment;
- shared expedition resources.

A companion keeps their own possessions when leaving.

## Equipment autonomy

Player preference can allow:

- autonomous purchases/upgrades;
- ask-before-major-purchase;
- player-assigned gear only.

Companions may commission real gear from the economy.

## Loot agreements

Possible party rules:

- player distribution;
- equal shares;
- need-based;
- wage-only hireling;
- keep-what-you-find.

Repeated unfairness can matter.

## Party tactics

Immediate commands:

- attack;
- hold;
- follow;
- move;
- regroup;
- retreat;
- do not attack;
- aid/downed ally.

Standing rules:

- protect injured;
- prioritize ranged enemies;
- don't chase;
- conserve healing;
- save rare abilities;
- avoid explosives near civilians;
- don't kill surrendered enemies;
- keep firing lanes clear.

## Formations

Potential formations:

- normal;
- tight;
- spread;
- column;
- defensive;
- ranged rear;
- stealth.

Friendly fire and weapon geometry make positioning important.

## Weapon-aware AI

AI should understand spear reach, bow sightlines, shield doorway control, AoE spacing, dagger close range, and dangerous blast weapons.

## Stealth parties

Stealth mode can ask companions to reduce noise, avoid sightlines, hold attacks, and maintain spacing.

A heavily armored or naturally clumsy companion may simply be poor at stealth.

## Tactical communication

Low-volume useful callouts can include:

- flanking;
- low ammunition;
- ally down;
- armor not penetrated;
- enemy behind;
- retreat.

Templates are sufficient; optional AI can vary wording later.

## Injury, capture, death

Companions use the same broad states:

- wounded;
- downed;
- dying;
- captured;
- dead.

Capture can create rescue content.

Permanent death can exist, but settings/guardrails should prevent pathfinding or technical jank from casually destroying a beloved long-term companion.

## Offscreen autonomy

Dismissed companions can work, train, craft, adventure, visit family, buy property, form relationships, accept contracts, become injured, or join other parties.

## Retirement

NPC adventurers may retire to become merchants, teachers, guards, craftsmen, settlers, or family members.

## Personal quests

Personal problems exist independently of arbitrary approval thresholds. Trust can determine whether a companion confides in the player.

## Romance

Romance is relationship-driven, not a persuasion check.

Attraction can depend on orientation/preferences, personality, culture, physical attraction, affection, trust, respect, and shared history.

Rejection can simply be rejection.

## NPC/NPC relationships

NPCs can become friends, rivals, partners, spouses, or family where appropriate. The world does not reserve every relationship for the player.

## Crime interaction

A companion who witnesses player crime may lie, stay silent, report, assist, become an accomplice, or leave.

Companions can also be arrested.

## Homes and labor

Companions can own/rent homes or live elsewhere. Hirelings can support guards, builders, farmers, craftsmen, scouts, healers, and caravan labor.

## Leadership and teaching

Leadership may improve large-group coordination without gating normal companionship.

Characters can teach skills, languages, cultural foundations, scientific concepts, or magical techniques.

## AI narrative connection

Simulation stores relationship truth and memories. Optional AI expresses them; it does not decide them.
