# OTHERREACH — Cosmology & World-Language Design

**Project codename:** UNNAMED  
**Working game title:** **Otherreach**  
**Purpose:** Capture the current cosmology, terminology, travel concepts, and naming ideas without prematurely locking undecided lore into canon.

---

## 1. Title

# OTHERREACH

The title can work on several levels at once:

- the frontier beyond the known world;
- the expanding influence or “reach” of the Other into ordinary reality;
- mortals learning to reach across impossible distances, worlds, and eras;
- exploration eventually extending beyond geography itself.

### Preserved tagline candidates

> **Beyond where. Beyond when.**

> **Every road leads somewhere. The Otherways do not.**

Both remain strong candidates.

---

## 2. The Other

At the center of the setting is a concept known simply as **the Other**.

The Other should not initially be defined as one simple alternate dimension. It may be:

- a layer outside ordinary reality;
- the medium between worlds;
- the space between places;
- the space between times;
- an underlying structure through which distance and chronology can fold;
- a collection of unreachable places, times, and realities;
- several different phenomena that civilizations incorrectly group under one name.

A major setting principle is that no culture necessarily possesses the complete explanation.

A scholar might describe dimensional topology.  
A priest might describe a divine boundary.  
A druid might speak of hidden roots joining distant places.  
A necromancer might believe death weakens attachment to ordinary reality.  
An ordinary traveler may simply know that some roads are wrong.

These interpretations can coexist.

---

## 3. The Veil

**The Veil** is the proposed name for the perceived boundary between ordinary reality and the Other.

It need not be a literal membrane. The Veil may be:

- naturally thin in certain locations;
- weakened by ancient events;
- affected by magic;
- damaged by repeated gate use;
- influenced by celestial or cosmological cycles;
- deliberately pierced or stabilized.

Different cultures may believe the Veil protects the world, imprisons it, or is merely an imperfect description of something they do not understand.

---

## 4. Otherfolds

An **Otherfold** is a distortion in normal space-time.

A fold could:

- connect distant physical locations;
- connect different eras;
- connect different realities;
- produce impossible interior geometry;
- temporarily shorten distance;
- arise naturally;
- be stabilized artificially;
- collapse or change.

This allows teleportation and similar travel to exist as part of the world's cosmology instead of as an unexplained convenience.

---

## 5. Otherways

**Otherways** are traversable routes associated with the Other.

They may pass through stabilized folds or through the Other itself.

A key line worth preserving:

> **No one knows where the Otherways lead. Some go elsewhere. Some go elsewhen.**

Possible characteristics:

- some are stable and mapped;
- some change destination;
- some open only under special conditions;
- some require spells, keys, artifacts, knowledge, or ritual;
- some contain hazards between entrance and destination;
- some permit cargo, mounts, companions, or caravans;
- some do not;
- some may be ancient infrastructure whose builders are unknown.

Otherways should feel like discoveries and pieces of the world, not merely fast-travel buttons.

---

## 6. Otherwhere

**Otherwhere** describes displacement outside ordinary geography.

It could refer to:

- another place in the same world;
- another world;
- another reality;
- a region that does not map cleanly onto normal space;
- places with impossible spatial relationships.

Example:

> “That gate does not lead north. It leads Otherwhere.”

---

## 7. Otherwhen

**Otherwhen** describes displacement outside ordinary chronology.

It need not mean simple backward/forward time travel. It could include:

- the past;
- the future;
- alternate historical branches;
- preserved moments;
- regions where time passes differently;
- places displaced from their original era.

The deliberate strangeness of the word is useful: **when becomes a destination just as where normally is.**

**Otherwhere** = outside normal geography.  
**Otherwhen** = outside normal chronology.

---

## 8. Othergates

**Othergates** are natural, constructed, or stabilized entrances into Otherways or Otherfolds.

They need not all look alike.

Possible forms:

- ancient arches;
- standing stones;
- magical doors;
- mechanical structures;
- roots;
- mirrors;
- wells;
- ruins;
- underground devices;
- invisible thresholds;
- structures that become gates only under specific conditions.

Different civilizations may have discovered different ways to interact with the same underlying phenomenon.

---

## 9. Otherhome

**Otherhome** is the leading candidate for the main central city, starting city, or first major settlement.

The name carries emotional meaning.

One possible origin is that displaced settlers founded a permanent refuge after losing access to their original home. It was not *home*, but it became another home — **Otherhome**.

Over time, the nickname may have replaced the city's original formal name.

This offers a good slow-burn reveal: the player can initially treat Otherhome as an ordinary fantasy city name, only later learning that the name itself is evidence of ancient displacement.

Possible roles:

- starting hub;
- major trade center;
- cosmopolitan city;
- meeting place of multiple races and cultures;
- repository of Other-related scholarship;
- city built around or near an ancient gate;
- symbolic center of civilization;
- home to factions that disagree about whether Other-travel should continue.

**Status:** strong candidate, not immutable canon.

---

## 10. Otherborn

**Otherborn** describes beings whose origin is fundamentally connected to the Other.

Possible meanings:

- born in another world;
- descended from people displaced through an Otherway;
- born in a region altered by the Other;
- originating from another timeline;
- belonging to a people whose ancestors came through the Other;
- born during a major cosmological event.

The term can also carry social meaning.

Depending on culture, Otherborn might be:

- honored;
- feared;
- considered blessed;
- considered cursed;
- treated as foreigners;
- treated as prophetic;
- subject to laws or prejudice;
- completely ordinary in cosmopolitan regions.

The word should feel like an in-world identity, not merely a biological category.

---

## 11. Othermarked

An **Othermarked** person was not necessarily born connected to the Other but was permanently changed by later exposure.

Possible effects:

- visible markings;
- altered magical affinity;
- unusual perception;
- ability to sense folds;
- temporal memory anomalies;
- unusual resistance or vulnerability;
- social or religious stigma.

Useful distinction:

**Otherborn** — origin is tied to the Other.  
**Othermarked** — altered later by contact with it.

---

## 12. Otherwrought

**Otherwrought** describes objects deliberately made using materials, forces, knowledge, or techniques associated with the Other.

Examples might include:

- weapons;
- armor;
- portal components;
- keys;
- relics;
- tools;
- impossible containers;
- legendary artifacts.

This is particularly useful for high-end crafting and epic quest rewards.

An Otherwrought item should feel materially different from an ordinary enchanted object.

---

## 13. Candidate Other-Materials

These remain exploratory vocabulary.

### Otherglass
A crystalline or glass-like substance associated with folds or spatial distortion.

Potential uses:
- portal lenses;
- magical optics;
- high-tier crafting;
- windows into distant places;
- revealing hidden folds.

### Otherstone
A mineral found near dimensional disturbances or brought from elsewhere.

Potential uses:
- gates;
- anchors;
- architecture;
- magical storage;
- settlement defenses.

### Othersteel
A possible high-tier alloy incorporating Other-derived material.

### Otherfire
A strange energy or flame associated with contact between realities.

It could behave differently from ordinary elemental fire and might power devices, reveal folds, or damage magical structures.

---

## 14. The Otherfall

**The Otherfall** is a strong candidate name for a foundational historical catastrophe.

Possible consequences:

- populations displaced;
- regions exchanged between realities;
- cities disappearing;
- fragments of different eras overlapping;
- new races arriving;
- impossible geography forming;
- monsters crossing into the world;
- civilizations collapsing;
- the founding of Otherhome;
- ancient Otherways becoming accessible.

The exact event should remain undecided until the larger history is built.

It may even turn out that “the Otherfall” is a later name applied to several separate events.

---

## 15. Otherreach as an In-World Term

The title **Otherreach** can also exist inside the setting.

Possible meanings:

### Geographic
A dangerous frontier where Other phenomena are common.

### Cosmological
The extent to which the Other can influence ordinary reality.

### Historical
A region permanently changed by the Otherfall.

### Scholarly
The measurable area around a fold in which ordinary physical rules become unstable.

### Poetic
The desire to reach beyond the limits of the known.

This gives the title room to gain meaning as the player learns the world's history.

---

## 16. Otherwake

An **Otherwake** could be the disturbance left behind after travel through the Other, like a ship's wake.

Possible effects:

- temporary magical instability;
- visible distortion;
- strange weather;
- time anomalies;
- creature attraction;
- traces that can be tracked.

This could support investigation, pursuit, detection, and concealment mechanics.

---

## 17. Othertide

**Othertide** suggests that the Other's influence rises and falls.

Possible causes:

- celestial cycles;
- magical cycles;
- large-scale gate use;
- unknown cosmological mechanics.

Possible gameplay consequences:

- some gates open only at certain times;
- rare materials appear;
- hidden areas become reachable;
- dangerous creatures emerge;
- magic behaves differently.

This could eventually support world events.

---

## 18. Otherstorm

An **Otherstorm** could be a violent local outbreak of unstable Other phenomena.

Potential effects:

- spontaneous gates;
- spatial distortion;
- gravity changes;
- temporal slips;
- strange weather;
- creatures or objects arriving from elsewhere.

**Status:** candidate phenomenon.

---

## 19. Otherdeep

The **Otherdeep** could describe regions far removed from ordinary reality.

It need not literally mean “below.”

Possible meanings:

- unmapped Otherways;
- dangerous layers beyond stable routes;
- regions where normal physical rules break down;
- places unreachable by ordinary gate networks.

This term has strong dark-fantasy potential.

---

## 20. Otherlight

**Otherlight** could describe energy, illumination, or perception associated with the Other.

Possible uses:

- portal glow;
- religious symbolism;
- magical energy;
- rare resource;
- light that reveals hidden spatial structures.

Currently undefined.

---

## 21. Otherbound

**Otherbound** might describe:

- someone destined for the Other;
- a traveler unable to remain permanently in one reality;
- someone magically linked to a gate;
- an order or faction devoted to Other exploration.

This remains a strong candidate for faction, quest-line, or character terminology.

---

## 22. Otherblood

**Otherblood** could be a cultural or genealogical term for ancestry associated with the Other.

It might be prestigious in one culture and insulting in another.

It should be used carefully so the cosmology does not become unnecessarily genetic.

---

## 23. Otherdream

An **Otherdream** might be:

- a vision;
- a memory from another timeline;
- communication across impossible distance;
- perception of another reality;
- contact with an unknown being.

This could support quests and lore without requiring a literal dream dimension.

---

## 24. Otherthen and Othernow

Possible scholarly or poetic terminology:

**Otherthen** — a past that is not our past; an alternate or displaced historical state.

**Othernow** — a present existing alongside the current one; a parallel or overlapping reality.

These should remain optional until deeper timeline mechanics are defined.

---

## 25. Otherplace

**Otherplace** works well as deliberately unsophisticated folk terminology.

A farmer may call something an Otherplace while academics argue about whether it is a pocket reality, a fold interior, or a displaced region.

That difference in language can help cultures feel real.

---

## 26. Otherrealm, Otherworld, Otherland, Otherspace

These are useful secondary terms but less distinctive as central names.

- **Otherrealm** — traditional fantasy language.
- **Otherworld** — immediately understandable but extremely common.
- **Otherland** — geographic, exploratory, old-fashioned.
- **Otherspace** — more science-fantasy or academic.

They may work best as regional or cultural synonyms.

---

## 27. Cultural Interpretations

There should not be one universally accepted explanation of the Other.

### Scholars
Reality has structure; folds connect normally separated coordinates.

### Arcane practitioners
The Veil is a magical boundary shaped by power and will.

### Druids
All places grow from hidden roots; Otherways follow those roots.

### Religions
The Other may be a divine realm, creation beyond creation, afterlife boundary, prison, or forbidden domain.

### Necromancers
Death may weaken a being's attachment to place and time, allowing contact with the Other.

### Folk belief
Some roads are wrong. Some doors should not be opened.

### Ancient technological interpretation
Devices described as magical gates may exploit physical laws modern cultures only partially understand.

None of these explanations needs to be fully correct.

---

## 28. Race and Culture Implications

Different races or cultures can have distinct travel traditions without permanently locking other characters out of them.

Examples:

- exceptional mounts;
- druidic root-paths;
- gate geometry;
- mechanical transit;
- natural fold navigation;
- religious prohibitions against Other-travel;
- rites of passage involving the Veil.

Race or culture might grant earlier access, different terminology, special quests, or unique expertise.

Major travel systems should generally remain discoverable or learnable by others later.

---

## 29. Travel Hierarchy

Future travel can be organized into five broad tiers.

### Tier 1 — Ordinary Movement
Walking, sprinting, climbing, swimming, roads, boats.

### Tier 2 — Faster Physical Travel
Mounts, carts, wagons, ships, culture-specific transport.

### Tier 3 — Extraordinary Physical Movement
Gliding, magical flight, flying creatures, unusual traversal abilities.

### Tier 4 — Point-to-Point Travel
Stable Othergates, known Otherways, rituals, portal infrastructure.

### Tier 5 — Space-Time Traversal
Unstable folds, Otherwhere, Otherwhen, deep Otherways, ancient gates, wormhole-like phenomena.

Higher tiers should feel earned, discovered, and understood rather than simply unlocked as menu conveniences.

---

## 30. Other-Travel Gameplay Principles

Other-travel should reinforce exploration.

Where practical:

- gates physically exist in the world;
- routes must be discovered;
- destinations must be learned;
- travel knowledge matters;
- some routes carry risk;
- gates may need repair or activation;
- rare routes may require special equipment or knowledge;
- cargo and companions matter;
- some routes cannot accommodate animals, wagons, or large groups.

The Other should make fast travel **more immersive**, not less.

---

## 31. Streaming and Loading

Otherways also provide a natural fiction for technical transitions.

Normal exterior travel should remain seamless wherever practical.

When loading boundaries are necessary, they can occur behind:

- caves;
- tunnels;
- doors;
- stairs;
- elevators;
- gates;
- magical thresholds;
- Otherways.

This keeps technical streaming boundaries from feeling arbitrary.

---

## 32. Relationship to Magic

The Other should not replace the game's many magic schools.

Instead, different schools can interact with the same cosmology in different ways.

Possible examples:

- teleportation manipulates folds;
- divination perceives possible Otherwhere or Otherwhen states;
- necromancy interacts with weakened attachment to ordinary reality;
- druidic magic uses natural networks that intersect the Other;
- healing restores a being toward its proper state;
- summoning temporarily bridges distant locations;
- protective magic reinforces the Veil.

These remain possibilities, not finalized mechanics.

---

## 33. Epic Quest Possibilities

The cosmology supports long-form epic quests naturally.

Examples:

- rediscover a lost gate network;
- follow a legendary traveler's route;
- recover pieces of an Otherwrought artifact;
- find a city displaced Otherwhere;
- discover why a historical figure appears centuries apart;
- stabilize a dangerous fold;
- choose whether to reopen a sealed Otherway;
- investigate an unexplained Otherwake;
- uncover the true history of Otherhome.

Potential rewards:

- unique travel access;
- Otherwrought equipment;
- permanent knowledge;
- rare crafting recipes;
- ability to perceive folds;
- access to hidden regions.

---

## 34. Settlements and the Other

Later in development, the cosmology can interact with the building/property system.

Potential possibilities:

- settlements near folds attract scholars, merchants, or cults;
- dangerous folds increase risk;
- stable gates increase trade value;
- player settlements can become travel hubs;
- governments regulate gates;
- religious factions forbid certain crossings;
- Other materials enable unusual construction.

These are later-game possibilities and do not alter the early prototype roadmap.

---

## 35. Mystery Principle

The Other should not be fully explained early.

At first:

- people know practical rules and local myths;
- scholars disagree;
- ancient gates may simply be accepted facts;
- common people may not know which stories are true.

Later:

- contradictions appear;
- ancient records reveal deeper history;
- Otherwhere and Otherwhen become increasingly important;
- the player learns that familiar locations such as Otherhome are directly tied to the cosmology.

The world should feel older than the player and only partially understood.

---

## 36. Naming Principle

The **Other-** prefix should be used deliberately.

Not every strange thing should be called Other-something.

A believable world should also contain:

- regional names;
- religious terms;
- scientific terms;
- obsolete names;
- slang;
- folk terminology.

The Other vocabulary works best if it feels like language that developed because people repeatedly encountered phenomena normal words could not adequately describe.

## 39. Open Questions

Do not answer these prematurely.

1. What exactly is the Other?
2. Was the Otherfall one event or several?
3. Did Otherhome's founders originate in this world?
4. Are Otherborn one specific people or a broad category?
5. Can living beings survive indefinitely inside the Other?
6. Are there beings or civilizations native to it?
7. Do Otherways have fixed destinations?
8. Can Othergates reach the actual past, or only displaced/alternate chronology?
9. How dangerous should Otherwhen travel become?
10. What prevents space-time travel from trivializing geography, death, quests, or consequences?
11. Who built the oldest Othergates?
12. Can players eventually construct or repair gates?
13. How do governments and religions regulate dimensional travel?
14. Does necromancy truly interact with the Other, or is that merely one interpretation?
15. Does the player begin Otherborn, become Othermarked, or initially have no special connection?
16. Should “the Otherreach” be a specific region, or should the title's meaning remain broader?

---

## 40. Current Direction

The strongest current framework is:

- **Otherreach** is the game.
- **Otherhome** is the leading candidate for the central/start city.
- **The Other** is the broad cosmological unknown.
- **The Veil** separates ordinary reality from it.
- **Otherfolds** distort space-time.
- **Otherways** make those distortions traversable.
- **Othergates** provide controlled access.
- **Otherwhere** describes impossible place.
- **Otherwhen** describes impossible time.
- **Otherborn** and **Othermarked** describe fundamentally different relationships between people and the Other.
- **Otherwrought** describes extraordinary artifacts and crafted objects.
- **The Otherfall** is a strong candidate for a foundational catastrophe.

The cosmology should support fantasy, science-fantasy, religion, mystery, travel, magic, exploration, crafting, settlements, and epic quests without forcing the setting into a single explanatory lens.

Above all:

> **The Other should feel older than the player, larger than any one civilization, and only partially understood.**
