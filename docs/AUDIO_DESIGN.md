# UNNAMED - Audio Design

Sonic identity for the races, regions and systems. Companion to `MYTHOLOGY.md` (the
cosmology), `RACES.md` (mechanics) and `ENDGAME_QUESTLINES.md` (the secrets).

Every score here is **generated original**, not licensed from a library. That is a
licensing decision first and an artistic one second: stock packs carry attribution
requirements, per-track redistribution limits and hidden non-commercial clauses, and
provenance across hundreds of cues is a liability. See `AUDIO_PIPELINE.md` for the
models and how to run them.

---

## Principles

**Sound is defined by relationship to Pacha.** The cosmology already organises the
races, so it organises the music: each people sounds like its position relative to
reality. A native sounds grounded, a migrant sounds displaced, an artificial people
sound *placed* rather than grown.

**Silence is a material.** The setting's horror is absence — a destroyed Pacha, a
missing page, a maker who left no name. Most cues carry more silence than sound.

**Nothing is orchestral by default.** Orchestral scoring reads as European fantasy.
This world's instruments should be plausible for its peoples to have built: stone,
gut, metal, breath, and things that are not instruments at all.

**Motifs are structural, not decorative.** A recurring figure should tell the player
something true about what they are looking at.

---

## The Pacha sound

The cosmological constant, used sparingly. A low, slowly beating drone in a non-Western
tuning — two tones a few cents apart so the beat frequency is felt rather than heard.
It means *reality itself* is present as a subject.

**A Pachakuti is not a new sound.** It is the existing soundscape **detuned, reversed
and resampled** — everything already playing is bent to the wrong pitch and played
backwards. The player should recognise their surroundings being *reinterpreted* rather
than replaced. This is the single most important sound in the game and it should never
be explained.

## Race identities

Each race gets: a core texture, a signature instrument, a vocal quality, and a
**deliberate absence** — the thing missing from their sound. The absence is what makes
them distinguishable at a glance.

### Veth — the touched lineage

| | |
|---|---|
| **Core** | Warm, unremarkable, acoustic. Fiddle, frame drum, breath, room tone. |
| **Instrument** | A battered wooden flute — the same instrument in every Veth cue, differently played. |
| **Voice** | Untrained, together, slightly out of tune with each other. Community, not chorus. |
| **Absence** | No low end. Veth music has no bass foundation, because they have never been sure the ground holds. |

The anchor. Every other race should be recognisable *as a departure from this*.

### Kal — the calcified

| | |
|---|---|
| **Core** | Struck stone and worked metal. Chisel on granite, hammer on anvil, the ring of a struck slab. |
| **Instrument** | Lithophone — tuned stone bars, one per note. Each Kal hold tunes its own set, so the same melody differs between holds. |
| **Voice** | Low, slow, few words, long gaps, and a distinct *end*: they stop rather than fade. |
| **Absence** | No sustained tone. Everything is struck and decays. Nothing in Kal music can continue by itself. |

Their terror in sound: a Kal cue ends by **petrifying** — the tempo slows, each note
lasts longer, until the last strike simply never resolves.

### Siann — the long view

| | |
|---|---|
| **Core** | Extremely long tones, minimal movement, hair-fine intonation. Bowed metal, glass harmonica, struck bowls. |
| **Instrument** | Bowed steel — long, patient, unwavering lines that outlast the scene. |
| **Voice** | Clear, unhurried, unsettlingly precise. They do not breathe audibly where a human would. |
| **Absence** | No rhythm section, no beat, no pulse. Nothing marks time, because time is not scarce to them. |

A Siann cue should make the player feel hurried by comparison.

### Orenth — the walkers

| | |
|---|---|
| **Core** | Movement. Footfall, wind, rope, the creak of a loaded pack. Percussion that sounds like walking. |
| **Instrument** | A hand drum pattern that is always the same tempo as a walking pace, and a breathy pipe that never quite settles into key. |
| **Voice** | Hoarse, breathless, as though speaking while climbing. |
| **Absence** | No resolution. Orenth cues modulate away and never return to the tonic. They are always departing. |

Their cost is audible: the longer an Orenth cue runs, the **thinner** it gets — layers
drop out and are not replaced, exactly as crossings hollow out the walker.

### Mor — the stolen

| | |
|---|---|
| **Core** | Everything pitched *slightly wrong*. Chords missing their third, instruments that stop abruptly, reverb with no source. |
| **Instrument** | A voice with no body — sung tones with the breath removed, so the note exists without the physical act of making it. |
| **Voice** | Words are audible but arrive with a delay of a fraction of a second, as if crossing a distance. |
| **Absence** | **No room tone.** Mor cues have no acoustic space around them, which is deeply uncomfortable and rarely noticed consciously. |

### Constructed — the written

| | |
|---|---|
| **Core** | Precision. Extremely accurate pitch and timing, clean envelopes, no human imprecision anywhere. |
| **Instrument** | Plucked strings at mathematically even intervals, and a pure sine that functions as a *cantus firmus*. |
| **Voice** | Perfectly in tune, perfectly timed, and subtly mechanical — not robotic, just *without error*. |
| **Absence** | No decay and no noise. Real instruments breathe and die away; Constructed sound begins and ends exactly. |

The unease comes from perfection. It should be beautiful and slightly unbearable.

### Vaskaal — the void-farers

| | |
|---|---|
| **Core** | **Not of this world's tuning.** Use a genuinely different temperament, so they are never in tune with anything else. |
| **Instrument** | Signal tones, interference, electrical hum, and percussion that sounds like something enormous moving far away. |
| **Voice** | Flat, unhurried, translated-sounding. Never sings. |
| **Absence** | No resonance with the Pacha drone. Every other race can be heard *against* it; the Vaskaal are inert to it. |

Their arrival should be the only time the game uses **silence followed by something
loud**. Everything else approaches.

### Ondrek — the read-stone

| | |
|---|---|
| **Core** | Very slow, very low, and warm. Stone played gently — palms, not hammers. |
| **Instrument** | Rubbed stone and a low subharmonic drone. Closer to breath than to percussion. |
| **Voice** | Extremely slow speech with long pauses, and a texture like a landslide heard from a distance. |
| **Absence** | **No percussion and no sharp attack.** The Ondrek never strike anything. They are the only people who work stone without hitting it. |

Contrast with the Kal is deliberate: the Kal **strike**, the Ondrek **rest on**. Same
material, opposite gesture. A player should be able to tell them apart with their eyes
closed.

## Regions

| Region | Sound |
|---|---|
| **Veth settlements** | Veth music plus market noise, animals, and children. The only place with ordinary human clutter. |
| **Kal holds** | Long reverb, footsteps that echo too far, and the tuned stone of the hold's own lithophone. |
| **Siann archives** | Near-silence. Paper, breath, one distant bowed line. No music cues — the drone is the score. |
| **Orenth routes** | Wind at the front of the mix, walking rhythm underneath, no melody. |
| **Ondrek sites** | Low rubbed stone, and the unsettling warmth of a place that is quietly inhabited. |
| **Vaskaal wreck** | Electrical hum, structural groans, and their incompatible tuning pressing against everything else. |
| **Old quarry and stone fields** | The Kal-petrification cue, ambient. Slow, struck, unresolved. |
| **Pachakuti sites** | The Pacha drone at its loudest, and any existing ambience played backwards. |

## Where music stops

Hard rule, and a design statement: **no music during exploration of a new region for
the first time.** The first visit is scored only by ambience. Music enters on the
*second* visit, so the player learns that hearing a theme means they are somewhere they
have been before — and a new theme means they have arrived somewhere new.

Music plays:
- in settlements and safe places
- during set-piece combat
- in the **ninth hall** and other memorials
- with each race's endgame revelation

Music does not play:
- during first-entry exploration
- during a Pachakuti, which is ambience only
- over any dialogue with a gated chain

## Implementation

| Property | Value | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Music | 48 kHz stereo, **Vorbis or Opus**, 256 kbps | Loops cleanly, small files |
| SFX | 48 kHz mono where positional, stereo for UI | Wwise/Godot positional audio expects mono |
| Ambience | 48 kHz stereo, **seamless loop** with a 2 s crossfade tail | Long beds, need gapless |
| Stems | Generate and **keep** music stems separately | Lets combat and calm variants reuse one generation |
| Naming | `<category>_<subject>_<variant>.ogg`, e.g. `music_kal_hold_calm.ogg` | Matches the asset pipeline convention |

**Stems are the key economy.** One ACE-Step generation of a track with drums, bass and
melody kept separately gives the combat mix (all layers), the town mix (drums muted)
and the tension mix (bass only) for the cost of one render. Generate with stems
whenever the model supports it.

## Production order

1. **Ambience beds** — one per region. Lowest risk, huge coverage, and they establish the palette.
2. **Race motifs** — one short cue per race, using the tables above. These become reusable building blocks.
3. **UI and interaction SFX** — inventory, confirmation, failure. Small and high-frequency.
4. **Combat SFX** — sword, arrow, impact, creature vocalisations. Pairs directly with the assets already built.
5. **The Pacha drone and the Pachakuti** — the signature sounds. Worth doing once, carefully.
6. **Location themes** — Kal hold, Siann archive, ninth hall.
7. **Endgame cues** — eight, one per chain, last because they need the most care.

## Open questions

1. **Do the races share a scale?** Using one modal system across all eight with different instruments would make the world cohere and make the Vaskaal's deliberate incompatibility meaningful.
2. **Does combat get its own language, or reuse race material?** Reuse is cheaper and connects the world; a separate combat palette is clearer.
3. **Is there a sung theme?** ACE-Step handles vocals, but lyrics in a fictional language need a decision on whether any lyrics are ever intelligible.
4. **Voice acting scope.** VibeVoice plus the existing 11 clips makes a full cast feasible, but every line is a generation — worth bounding before starting.
