# UNNAMED - The Stone Question

A major faction conflict and multi-stage questline. The Kal stone is not a resource to
be gathered or a relic to be revered; it is the subject of a disagreement between two
peoples who each have a real claim and cannot both be satisfied.

Design intent: **no correct answer.** The player should finish the chain having chosen
a side, not having solved a puzzle.

---

## The Ondrek — eighth race, the read-stone

**Aptitude: science, craft. Balance: composition, memory, slow.**

Beings of stone that stand and think and remember. Not Kal. The Ondrek say they are
what the Kal become; the Kal say that is a lie told to justify a quarry.

The position is genuinely contested in-world, and the player will find scholars on
both sides.

The Ondrek are **not** a grotesque. They are courteous, patient and profoundly sad,
and their argument is not that Kal stone is worthless. Their argument is that they do
not know, and that the Kal do not know either, and that the Kal have chosen
certainty over evidence.

They are the finest stoneworkers alive because stone is not material to them. It is
relatives. They read it the way a scribe reads a page, and they can tell the difference
between stone that was once Kal and stone that never was — a claim the Kal dispute,
because accepting it would mean accepting that some ancestors are not in the quarries.

**Why they quarry.** The Ondrek are ending. They do not reproduce; they simply stop
emerging, and each generation of new read-stone is rarer than the last. Whatever makes
a thinking stone is failing. Stone is the only medium that has ever carried memory,
and the only place they have ever tried to make more of themselves.

They are quarrying the Kal dead because they believe it is the only route to
continuing, and because they believe — with evidence the player can weigh — that the
stone they are taking was never anyone.

**Mechanically:** stonesense, material memory, unrivaled craft. No magic. Heavy,
slow, very hard to kill, genuinely poor at stealth and social manoeuvring in cultures
that will not speak to them.

---

## The shape of the conflict

Three parties, and the player can disappoint all of them.

**The Kal** regard the quarries as graves and their study as desecration. They will not
permit it and they will not negotiate the principle, only the mechanics. A Kal who
concedes that some stone was never Kal concedes that some of the dead are not there,
and that is a religious crisis rather than a debate.

**The Ondrek** accept the Kal's grief as real and dismiss the Kal's certainty as
unearned. They need the stone and they are dying. From where they stand, the Kal are
refusing to examine evidence because the conclusion frightens them.

**Everyone else** buys the result. Stone-crafted goods are the finest made, the
Ondrek trade generously, and most settlements would rather not examine where the
stone came from. The player will find merchants, priests and nobles who have decided
the question is complicated and would prefer it stayed that way.

## Questline: succession of the ninth hall

Written as the bones of a multi-session chain, in the charter's preferred shape —
research, travel, difficult acquisition, a real choice, no clean resolution.

### I. A stone that was not a stone
An old quarry is being worked. The Kal have known for months and cannot stop it; the
player encounters the dispute rather than being sent to it.

*Player learns:* there is an argument. Both sides have a case. Nothing is settled.

### II. The reader's testimony
The Ondrek offer to demonstrate their discrimination — to show the player two stones,
one from a quarry they are working, one from a Kal memorial, and say which is which.

They can. It is convincing. It is also exactly what a party with a financial interest
would claim to be able to do, and a Kal scholar will point that out.

*Player learns:* the Ondrek's central claim may be true. It cannot be verified without
methods the Kal refuse to permit.

### III. The disputed method
Establishing the truth requires instruments — a method of reading stone that neither
side currently has. It sits in a place neither can reach: a Kal archive that has been
sealed, an Ondrek quarry the Kal have blockaded, or an older site from before the last
Pachakuti.

*Player does:* the difficult acquisition. Should involve the surviving architecture of
an earlier world, and probably a Pachakuti site, since that is where Kal stone comes
from.

### IV. What the method is for
The instruments would settle the question in one direction or the other, permanently.
Whoever holds them first decides something enormous, and both parties know it.

The player can:
- hand them to the Kal (the quarries close; the Ondrek begin to die out, visibly)
- hand them to the Ondrek (the quarries expand; the Kal fracture religiously)
- keep them, and be hunted by both
- destroy them, which ends the argument by ending the possibility of knowing, and is
  the option both parties will immediately agree is the worst one

### V. Succession
Whichever way IV resolved, the Ondrek are still ending. The final stage is about what
they do instead, and the player's earlier choices determine which options exist.

Possible resolutions, in rough order of how hard they are to reach:
- **A negotiated succession** — the Kal help the Ondrek; the Ondrek stop needing Kal
  stone; both lose something and gain something. Hardest, requires trust earned across
  the whole chain.
- **The Ondrek end** — they stop emerging, and the world becomes slightly less. No
  villain, no victory.
- **A new origin** — the Ondrek find a way to make more of themselves that does not
  require the dead. Expensive, uncertain, and possibly produces something that is not
  quite Ondrek.
- **The ninth hall** — a memorial neither side expected, holding the names of every
  Kal stone taken and every Ondrek that stopped waking. It settles nothing and is the
  only ending both sides will attend.

### VI. Aftermath hooks
- If the quarries continue, Kal PCs meet hostility in Ondrek towns and the reverse.
- The instruments, if kept, become a faction target for the rest of the game.
- A destroyed method leaves a permanent question and a small group of scholars on
  both sides who never forgive the player.
- The Ondrek, if they succeed, become a faction that remembers who helped.

---

## Why this belongs in the game

It uses the setting's own cosmology rather than a bolted-on moral choice: the Kal
petrify because of Pacha transitions, which is the central phenomenon of the world.
It gives the Ondrek a reason to exist that is not villainy. And it gives every
non-Kal, non-Ondrek character a chance to be quietly complicit, which is the kind of
thing a persistent world should contain.

It also has no required outcome, so it survives the player ignoring it entirely —
the quarries simply keep working, and the world simply gets a little poorer.

## Open questions

1. How old is the Ondrek decline? Recent onset is an emergency; ancient decline is a
   quiet grief that changes the tone completely.
2. Do the Ondrek have a homeland, or are they diaspora living in stone structures
   other races built and abandoned?
3. Is the "ninth hall" ending reachable without the instruments, as the outcome of
   having chosen nothing?
4. Does the player ever meet a Kal who volunteers for the quarry — a Kal who believes
   the Ondrek are right about them?
