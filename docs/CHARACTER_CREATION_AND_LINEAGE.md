# OTHERREACH — Character Creation & Lineage

**Status:** Strong working direction  
**M2 impact:** None

## 1. Core model

Separate what RPGs often collapse into “race + class.”

A character is defined by:

- **Lineage** — biological/metaphysical ancestry;
- **Morphology** — physical expression/body plan;
- **Innate traits** — biological/metaphysical capabilities;
- **Origin** — where they were raised;
- **Culture** — traditions/knowledge that shaped them;
- **Background** — prior work/life;
- **Aptitudes** — personal tendencies;
- **Starting archetype** — adventuring training at game start;
- **Skills/knowledge** — learned during play;
- **Reputation/relationships** — social state.

## 2. Starting choices determine beginning, not destiny

A starting archetype should answer:

> What are you prepared to do on day one?

It should not answer:

> What are you allowed to become forever?

## 3. Biology vs culture

Character creation must distinguish:

### Innate biology
Cannot normally be learned:
- wings;
- extra articulation;
- ethereal existence;
- unique sensory structures;
- synthetic morphology;
- genuine inability to interface with magic.

### Inherited aptitude
Provides a starting/learning advantage but usually does not prohibit outsiders.

### Cultural knowledge
Languages, science, crafting, combat traditions and mystical systems learned from society.

A person of one lineage raised in another culture may know that culture better than someone of the lineage raised elsewhere.

## 4. Origin

Origin can affect:

- starting location;
- known maps;
- language;
- contacts;
- local reputation;
- environmental familiarity;
- basic skills.

## 5. Background

Examples:

- soldier;
- hunter;
- farmer;
- smith apprentice;
- merchant;
- miner;
- healer;
- scholar;
- drifter;
- sailor.

Background provides starting competence/knowledge, not permanent class locks.

## 6. Hybrid lineage

**Not final canon until compatibility matrix is written.**

Humanoid appearance does not guarantee reproductive compatibility.

Pairings may be:

- naturally compatible;
- compatible with assistance;
- viable but infertile;
- incompatible;
- extraordinary-only.

Examples already raised as open questions:

- Siann/Vaskaal;
- Kal/Veth.

## 7. Trait expression

Hybrids should not average parent stats.

Use trait expression:

- some traits inherited from parent A;
- some from parent B;
- some mutually exclusive;
- some unique interactions.

Armor fit follows body plan/morphology rather than the word “hybrid.”

## 8. Mutation and enhancement

Future character development may include:

- beneficial mutation;
- magical alteration;
- Vaskaal technology;
- Constructed modification;
- Othermarking;
- alchemical/biological change.

Examples:

- secondary arms;
- tail;
- enhanced eyes;
- reinforced skeleton;
- phase limb;
- altered respiration.

Every meaningful enhancement should carry cost/tradeoff.

## 9. Appearance

Character creation must respect each race's silhouette and morphology instead of mapping every race onto one human slider set.

Shared body-plan families can reuse tooling while preserving:

- proportions;
- joints;
- skin/material;
- sensory structures;
- wings;
- faces;
- hands/feet.

## 10. Knowledge barriers

A human learning Vaskaal science should not receive an arbitrary permanent racial XP penalty.

More interesting barriers are:

- language;
- prerequisite concepts;
- teachers;
- tools;
- access;
- cultural trust.

Once foundations are learned, progression can depend on the character's actual aptitude and effort.

## 11. Open questions

- Which lineages can hybridize naturally?
- Which enhancements are selectable at creation versus acquired in play?
- How much starting culture may differ from lineage?
- How many origin/background choices should the first playable build support?
- Which traits are true biology versus cultural convention?
