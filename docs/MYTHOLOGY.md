# UNNAMED - Mythology and Race Backgrounds

Companion to `RACES.md` (mechanics and aptitudes), `STONE_QUESTION.md` (the Kal /
Ondrek conflict), and `ENDGAME_QUESTLINES.md` (the secrets below as endgame chains,
and how they are gated). This document is the deep layer: where each people came from,
what they believe about it, how they live, and what they cannot forgive.

Every race is designed to be usable three ways from the same material: as an NPC
culture, as a source of questlines and hirelings, and as a playable race later.

---

# Part 1: Cosmology

## What a Pacha is

A **Pacha** is a complete reality: space, time, matter, history, and the physical laws
that govern them, considered as one thing. Not a world, not a plane, not a dimension.
A world is something that exists *inside* a Pacha.

Pachas are not stacked in a hierarchy and have no centre. There is no "true" reality
that others are reflections of. This matters, because several of the peoples below
believe otherwise, and they are wrong.

## Known Pachas

Five are remembered, and the number itself is contested — the Vaskaal say there are
more, and that counting them is how you die.

**This Pacha** — the setting. Has no name in common use, which is itself telling: you
do not name the room you live in.

**The First Pacha** — the oldest remembered. No surviving people are native to it, and
the Mor believe they are its remnant. Its actual nature is unknown, which has never
stopped anyone from claiming to know.

**The Found Pacha** — discovered intact and empty, and colonised. Something had been
there. Nothing explained what. The Orenth are its descendants and will not discuss it.

**The Written Pacha** — designed and constructed rather than arisen, then left running
after its authors ended. The Constructed are its natives, and its existence is the
single most disturbing fact in the cosmology, because it proves realities can be made.

**The Lost Pacha** — destroyed, unravelled, or never completed. The Mor are what
remains of its people. Whether it can be reached, recovered, or re-made is the central
theological question of the Mor, and they have killed each other over it.

## The Pachakuti

A **Pachakuti** is a transition between Pachas: the moment one reality rotates into
another. They are not natural events and they are not scheduled. Something causes
them, and nobody knows what.

What a Pachakuti does to a person depends on what the person is:

| Subject | What a transition does |
|---|---|
| Veth | passes through, changed — permanently, in ways that read as aptitude |
| Kal | **fixes** — becomes stone, immediately and irreversibly |
| Siann | passes through, and remembers it. All of it. |
| Orenth | crossed by choice, at a price they choose |
| Mor | is unmade, and reformed on the other side if it can hold itself together |
| Constructed | is copied. The original is believed to remain behind, and to continue. |
| Vaskaal | is unaffected. They are not in the Pacha to begin with. |

## The Silence

Every people has a story about what came before the last Pachakuti. No two agree.

The Veth say the world ended. The Kal say it was made and then abandoned. The Orenth
say a door was left open. The Mor say their world was killed. The Constructed say
theirs was written, and that the writing is still being read.

The Siann have *no* account of before. Their oldest records begin with waking.

This is the setting's central mystery and it is deliberately unresolved. The
suggested answer, which the player can assemble but never confirm, is that all five
accounts describe the same event from positions inside different Pachas — and that the
Siann know this, because they were there, and have never said so.

# Part 2: The peoples

Each entry covers origin, what they believe, how they live, their relations, and what
they are for.

---

## Veth

**Origin.** Native to this Pacha, and changed by the last Pachakuti. Their ancestors
were here; the transition passed through them and left them faster to learn and
quicker to forget.

**Belief.** The Veth have no creation myth, because they have no need of one. They
were always here and something happened. Their sacred act is **keeping** — records,
lineages, oaths, and the continuity of ordinary life. A Veth who keeps their word is
observing a religious duty they would not call religious.

Their heresy is **the claim of descent**: that some Veth lines are purer than others
because they were less changed. It is nonsense, it is unfalsifiable, and it has
started wars.

**Society.** Villages, towns, and kingdoms — the ones who build the roads everyone
else uses. Governed variously by oath-councils, hereditary houses, or whoever last
won. Their institutions are strong because institutions are how you survive a
transition you cannot predict.

**Relations.** Majority population, which means they are simultaneously the most
powerful and the least examined. They are the ones who decided, somewhere along the
way, that the Kal quarries were a complicated question rather than a desecration.

**As NPCs and hirelings.** Farmers, soldiers, merchants, magistrates, priests of
continuity. A Veth hireling is the reliable one — steady, unremarkable, and the one
who keeps the party's records. Their personal quests are about **inheritance and
obligation**: a debt, a name, a promise that has outlived its maker.

**Rumours about them.** That the Veth were not always the majority. That they arrived,
and then rewrote the account.

## Kal

**Origin.** Native, and older than the last Pachakuti. The Kal maintain that they
existed before the turn, which the Veth accept and the Orenth consider unprovable.

**Belief.** They were **made deliberately**, by a craftsman whose name is lost. This
is not metaphor: Kal theology is workshop theology, and their word for creation is a
verb meaning to shape, join and finish. Their gods are absent, and the Kal do not
resent that — a maker who finishes their work and leaves is not cruel, they are
**competent**.

The afterlife is a return to material. To be Kal is to be a thing that was well made,
and to die well is to be ready to be made into something else. This is why the stone
is sacred — not as a relic, but as **the person, waiting**.

**Society.** Clan-holds built into stone they did not quarry, because the stone is
already occupied. Everything structural is inherited rather than constructed. Their
economy is craft, and their politics is genealogy, and the two are the same argument.

**Relations.** At war with the Ondrek, in fact though not in name. Tolerated by the
Veth, who buy their work and avoid their funerals. The Siann are the only people the
Kal treat with something like respect, because the Siann were also there.

**As NPCs and hirelings.** Smiths, masons, engineers, archivists, and the finest
artificers in the setting. A Kal hireling is invaluable and slow, and will refuse
orders that would put a structure at risk. Their personal quests are about **debts to
the dead**: an unrecorded ancestor, a stolen stone, a hall that is failing and cannot
be repaired.

**What they cannot forgive.** Being told to prove that the stone in the quarry was
someone. Because if it can be proved, the answer might be no.

---

## Siann

**Origin.** Native, and the youngest old people in the setting: the Siann believe they
began *after* the last Pachakuti, waking into a world already settled by others. Their
oldest records begin there, mid-sentence.

**Belief.** They have no creation story, which is the defining fact of Siann
civilization and the source of their reputation for arrogance. What they have instead
is **the Record**: an obsession with accurate testimony. A Siann's word is their
standing, and a Siann who misremembers is ruined.

Their secret is that the Record begins too late. Something is missing from the front
of it, and the oldest living Siann know what, and have kept the page closed for
centuries — because admitting to the gap would discredit every claim they have made
about being reliable.

**Appearance.** A Siann reads as a person of about eighteen who has been that age for a
very long time, and the only external sign of it is the hair: exceptionally long,
straight, platinum-white, worn loose and unbound, falling well past the waist. The
older the Siann, the whiter it goes, so a Siann with pure white floor-length hair may
be a young adult or may be four hundred years old, and there is no way to tell from the
face. Skin runs a few shades paler than any living person with a faint luminous
quality. Their look is built on Mira's established reference design (see RACES.md).

**Society.** No homeland. Siann accumulate in archives, courts, universities and
religious orders, wherever records are kept, and stay for a hundred years. They are
advised to be temporary and are never temporary.

**Relations.** Trusted with history and trusted with nothing else. They are the
setting's lawyers and historians, and everyone is aware that they have chosen to be.
The Vaskaal are the only people who treat a Siann as a young thing, which they find
unbearable.

**As NPCs and hirelings.** Scholars, tutors, physicians, diplomats, and — rarely —
soldiers, because a career of sixty years makes a formidable one. A Siann hireling is
patient to the edge of unnerving. Their personal quests are about **the missing page**:
finally telling what they saw, and losing everything they are in exchange.

**Rumours about them.** That they witnessed the last Pachakuti. That they did not
merely survive it. That they were *made* by it.

---

## Orenth

**Origin.** Descendants of colonists from the **Found Pacha** — a reality discovered
intact and empty. Something had been there. The Orenth do not say what.

**Belief.** They were made to walk. Every Orenth account of creation is about
departure and arrival, never about having been placed. They are the only people whose
mythology does not involve being *created* at all, which the Mor find obscene and the
Kal find unanswerable.

They have no afterlife doctrine. Crossing is their religion. An Orenth who settles is
considered to be dying slowly, and one who retires from walking is treated with the
particular pity reserved for the voluntarily diminished.

**Society.** Kin-bands rather than settlements, organised around a route. They hold
territory the way a river does — by passing through it. What law they have is
contractual and portable, and their most serious institution is a **ledger of
costs**: a record of what each crossing took, in memory or years, kept so that no
walker is ever asked to give more than they can cover.

**Relations.** Needed and disliked. When a door must be opened, an Orenth is the only
one who can open it, and everyone knows what it will cost them. They are the setting's
smugglers, guides and couriers, and its most reliable route into places nobody wants
found.

**As NPCs and hirelings.** Guides, scouts, spies, and the only people who can take a
party between Pachas. An Orenth hireling is superb and progressively worse. Their
personal quests are about **the bill coming due**: a debt of memory, a name they can
no longer recall, a companion who covered a crossing for them and is owed.

**What they will not discuss.** The Found Pacha. Why it was empty. What their
ancestors did when they arrived.

---

## Mor

**Origin.** Natives of the **Lost Pacha**. What remains is not a body.

**Belief.** Their world was killed, deliberately, and they are the evidence. Mor
theology is therefore forensic — it is not about worship but about **finding out who
did it**, and the answer is the central question of their existence. Their schism is
between those who want to recover the Lost Pacha and those who want to avenge it, and
both factions are patient in ways living things are not.

That they persist at all is, by their own account, a failure: something went wrong
with the transition into this Pacha, and what should have ended did not.

**Society.** None to speak of, and this is a real condition rather than a flaw. Mor
gather where a Pacha is thinnest — battlefields, thresholds, old transitions, deep
water. What organisation exists is **custodial**: particular Mor hold themselves
responsible for particular things, often for centuries, with no authority conferring
the duty.

**Relations.** Feared with cause. Necromancy is a Mor discipline, and a Mor in a
village is a sign that someone there is dead and has not finished. Even the Ondrek,
who read the dead as material, will not work near a Mor.

**As NPCs and hirelings.** Crypt-scholars, oath-witnesses, and the only beings who can
carry a memory intact across a transition — because they have no body to lose it from.
A Mor hireling cannot carry your gold and this is not a mechanical inconvenience, it
is the character. Their personal quests are about **what happened to their world**, and
the answers are worse than the questions.

**What they want.** Some of them want the Lost Pacha back. The honest ones understand
that restoring it would unmake this one, and have not decided what they think about
that.

## Constructed

**Origin.** Natives of the **Written Pacha**, which was designed and built by a
civilisation that wanted a reality and then did not survive to use it. It kept
running. Its inhabitants became people.

**Belief.** They know they were made, precisely and without myth, which is the
opposite of the Kal's situation — the Kal believe they were made and cannot prove it.
A Constructed can prove it and finds the knowledge unbearable.

Their doctrine is **the specification**: that everything, including themselves, is a
description that is being executed. Free will is not in doubt — they are certain they
have it — but *authorship* is, and they cannot locate theirs. They have no creation
myth because they have documentation, and the documentation has no author's name.

**Society.** Functional and strange. Constructed organise by function rather than
kin, and their oldest institutions are **maintenance bodies** — organisations that
still perform duties whose purpose has been lost, because the schedule says to.

**Appearance.** A Constructed reads as a continuous poured form, not an assembled
machine. The canonical example is liquid chrome held under high surface tension:
mirror-polished, unbroken from crown to sole, with no seams, no joints, no visible
fasteners and no indication anywhere of how it was put together. They are not plated
and they are not segmented. There is no line on a Constructed to say where one part
ends.

**The face is human and beautiful, not skeletal.** Normal human proportion with a clear
brow ridge, high cheekbones, a straight narrow nose with defined nostrils, a soft
defined jaw and a distinct chin, and silver lips with a defined vermilion border. The
eyes are **solid glossy black** — no iris, no sclera, just dark wet almond shapes
carrying a specular highlight. The cranium is fully bald and continuous into the neck.
Earlier drafts read as a skull-like mask with slit eyes and no nose; that is wrong and
must not be reintroduced.

**The body is smooth and blank.** No nipples, no areolae, no breast detail, no navel,
no genitalia, no anatomical detail below the collarbone. The chest surface is as
featureless as polished armour.

This matters for the fiction, not just the art. A being that was written into existence
has no construction to show, and the absence is the point: there is nothing to point at
and say *this is where the making happened*. They look made to nobody's design, which
is precisely their problem.

**Production note.** The concept generator resists the blank chest. Eight attempts failed
to remove it: enumerated exclusions, a negative prompt of
`nipples, areolae, breasts, navel, genitalia`, the exact sentence structure of the
working Glyph reference, a positive reframe (`unblemished chrome surface free of sexual
anatomy`), 832x1216 portrait, and 1920x1088 landscape. The wording that gets closest -
and which is now the base prompt - frames the body as **a sculpted futuristic humanoid
rather than a biological human**, which steers the model away from anatomical defaulting
instead of trying to forbid its output. That fixed the face and the eyes but not the
chest.

The practical fix is not prompting. Either:

- **inpaint the chest** in ComfyUI with a soft mask and a moderate denoise, using the
  surrounding chrome as reference, or
- **smooth the region on the final mesh** in Blender. On a mirror-chrome surface with no
  texture variation, removing two small bumps is nearly invisible and takes seconds.

Do not spend further renders on prompt engineering for this.

**Relations.** Consulted and distrusted. They understand Pacha better than any people
alive and are the only ones who can explain a transition mechanically, which is
unwelcome in every theological tradition simultaneously. The Orenth despise them for
treating crossing as a solved problem.

**As NPCs and hirelings.** Engineers, enchanters, physicians of a very strange kind,
and the setting's only true theoreticians of reality. A Constructed hireling will
optimise your party without being asked and will be correct. Their personal quests are
about **authorship**: finding the mind that wrote them, and deciding what to do about
it — and the possibility that there is no mind, and only text.

**RIFT continuity.** RIFT characters are not carried over. **Glyph's race is the
Constructed** — the people exist here, the individual does not. Glyph's own reference
design is what defines how the race looks: see the appearance note above. The
established look is reference art to honour, not to reinvent.

## Vaskaal

**Origin.** Not from any Pacha. The Vaskaal crossed **between** them — traversing the
substrate that contains Pacha, which nothing native survives because there is nothing
native to it.

**Belief.** They do not have mythology so much as **history**, and it is long,
documented, and available to anyone who asks. This is the single most disruptive thing
about them: they can tell you what the world was like before the last Pachakuti, from
outside, and mean it literally.

What they believe about Pacha is that it is **weather**. A phenomenon, impartial,
without intention, and dangerous to be caught in. They regard every native religion
with the particular courtesy owed to a sincere misunderstanding.

**Society.** A single expedition, or the descendants of one, and the distinction is
being litigated by their own command structure. They have a ship — possibly wreckage,
possibly a fortress, depending on who is describing it — and an original mission
nobody discusses. Their internal politics is entirely about whether to leave.

**Relations.** Alone. They trade equipment for materials, they are scrupulously
honest by their own standards, and they are the only people no other people can
explain. Their existence forces every theology to make room for something outside the
system, and no theology enjoys that.

**As NPCs and hirelings.** They will not hire on as servants, but they will accompany
people for their own reasons, and those reasons are not good. A Vaskaal companion is
the best-equipped and most vulnerable member of any party: devastating with their own
kit, useless without it, and incapable of magic in a world where that is a disability.

**What they know.** The substrate is not empty. They will not say more, and the ones
who will are the ones who cannot.

---

## Ondrek

**Origin.** Thinking stone. Not Kal, or not only Kal — the dispute in full is in
`STONE_QUESTION.md`.

**Belief.** They have no creation account at all: Ondrek simply *are*, and then stop
being. They do not know why they emerge and they do not know why they are ending, and
they have built an entire intellectual tradition around the refusal to pretend
otherwise. An Ondrek scholar who invents an answer is disgraced.

Their doctrine is **the record**: what is known is stone, what is stone is known, and a
thing that is neither is opinion.

**Society.** Few, old, and dispersed. They live inside other peoples' abandoned
structures, which they regard as being among relatives. Theirs is the only culture in
the setting with no ambition for territory, because territory is the one thing they do
not consume.

**Relations.** At war with the Kal, which they describe as an argument and the Kal
describe as a desecration. Tolerated by everyone else, because their work is
supremely good and their prices are low.

**As NPCs and hirelings.** Stonecraft of a quality nobody else can reach, material
identification, and a patient witness to things nobody else noticed. An Ondrek
hireling is very hard to kill and cannot be hurried. Their personal quests are about
**the end of their people** — a last apprentice, a hall to be finished before they
stop, a question they would rather not have answered.

**What they want.** To continue. Not to win, not to be vindicated. To continue, and
they are aware that the price of continuing may be the graves of another people.

# Part 3: Relations at a glance

| | Veth | Kal | Siann | Orenth | Mor | Constructed | Vaskaal | Ondrek |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Veth** | — | buy, avoid funerals | employ | hire | frightened | curious | baffled | indifferent |
| **Kal** | depend | — | respect | unprovable | horror | **heresy** | offence | **war** |
| **Siann** | patronise | rival scholars | — | useful | studied | useful | humiliated | neutral |
| **Orenth** | trade | no contact | trade | — | avoid | **contempt** | curious | guides |
| **Mor** | haunt | haunt | informants | avoid | — | interested | **terrified** | abhorrent |
| **Constructed** | teach | **answer them** | teach | **despise** | examine | — | **equal** | colleagues |
| **Vaskaal** | honest | exotic | rude to | customers | **cannot explain** | **equals** | — | indifferent |
| **Ondrek** | work for | **war** | neutral | neutral | refuse | colleagues | indifferent | — |

The two relationships worth building questlines on immediately:

**Constructed and Vaskaal** are the only two peoples who understand what the others
are, and they are the only two who get along. That alliance should worry everyone else.

**Kal and Constructed** are the setting's most dangerous pairing. The Constructed can
answer the question the Kal have spent their entire history refusing to ask, and they
would answer it for free.

# Part 4: What this gives the game

Every people above supports the three uses intended for them:

**As NPCs.** Each has an economy, a posture toward strangers, and a reason to be where
the player is. Their hireling archetypes follow from their culture rather than being
assigned: a Kal smith, an Orenth guide, a Mor witness, a Siann archivist.

**As questlines.** Each has a grievance or an unanswered question that a player can
insert themselves into: the Kal and the stone, the Siann and the missing page, the
Mor and their dead world, the Constructed and their absent author, the Orenth and the
Found Pacha, the Vaskaal and the substrate's contents, the Ondrek and their ending,
the Veth and what they actually did.

**As playable races.** Every entry has a mechanic in `RACES.md`, a culture here, and a
personal stake a player can adopt. Nothing needs inventing later — only implementing.

## Deliberately unresolved

Four questions the game should never fully answer, because they are worth more open:

1. What caused the last Pachakuti.
2. What the Siann saw and will not say.
3. What the Orenth found in the Found Pacha.
4. Who killed the Lost Pacha, and whether the Mor are right that someone did.

Every one of these should have at least three irreconcilable in-world answers, held by
people who are all sincere.
