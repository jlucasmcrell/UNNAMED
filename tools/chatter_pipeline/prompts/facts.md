# Facts a chat line may stand on

Version 1 (2026-10-18). Everything here comes from `content/` in the game repository. A line may use these facts and no
others. If a line needs a fact that is not here, the line does not get written.

## The four places (the only places a line may be tagged with)

- **Ashen Hollow** - the waystation in its own small valley, north-west of the valley floor. A road stop: a forge shed,
  a lodge, a steward's house, a market stall holding what travellers going out to the old workings need (arrows,
  salves, and the iron the smith works). It makes no hides of its own. New arrivals come in by its Waystone.
- **Charwood Verge** is inside Ashen Hollow's valley, north-east: woods with wolves' den country, a ruined cart, a
  patch where woundmoss grows.
- **Blackvein Cut** is the old quarry, south-west: an iron seam in the rock, worked on and off.
- **Foldscar Ruin**, south-east: three stones standing at its heart. The Survey wants them set right. People keep away.
- **Cairnwold** - a moorland village on the dry south-west rise of a basin: the Long Lodge (a long communal hall on the
  village's east side), seven houses, drystone walls, a smithy with a forge and anvil, the well, and the staddle
  granary at the quiet west end (grain kept up on staddle stones, out of the wet and the rats).
- Around Cairnwold, in its basin: the North Ash Stand (ash wood, north side), the South Crags and the South Crag Seam
  (iron, along the south edge), the Sinking Moss (a low wet bog in the north-east), the Outfall (the cut on the east
  edge where the basin's water leaves).
- Cairnwold's market is a moorland village's: hides, ash, and moor gear. **Its own iron is running out, so iron is
  dear there** and carters bring it in along the road.
- **The Old East Road** - the road between the waystation and Cairnwold's basin. Along it: the **Lantern Shrine** (a
  stone and a lit post where travellers leave what they can spare; the watch keeps the lantern filled), the
  **Overturned Wain** (a wain on its side, its load half spoiled, and nobody came back for it), the **Sunk Barrow** (a
  broken ring of standing beams and two boulders round a stone cist on the high ground north of the road, kept by
  husks), and **the Rim** (the cliff top where the road meets the drop into the basin, with a watch-fire and a camp).
- **The wilds** - everything off the settlements and off the road: the moor, the woods, the bog, the crags.

## Work and trades

- Smiths work iron: billets, spear heads, moor gear, hinges, hoops. Iron out of a seam comes up uneven - soft in one
  stretch, hard as flint in the next. Bad iron shows up in the hammering.
- Farmers: fields, gardens, a granary. Grain on staddles, stored dry, counted as it comes in.
- Herders: flocks, hens, a cow. Hens stop laying, lambs come early, a beast goes missing.
- Hunters: traps on the moor and in the woods - snares, deadfall - hides, pelts, meat, and knowing where a thing
  crossed. The small pelts go to the granary keeper's trade.
- Guards and the watch: gates, walls, the road stop, the watch-fire on the Rim, the lantern kept lit.
- Carters: goods along the road between the towns; a cart is a day's work to right and a broken axle ends a trip.
- Labourers: digging, hauling, cutting timber, working a seam. Hands, backs, and a job that is never finished.
- Crafters: bench, forge, loom, working for someone else. Repetition, small errors, sore wrists.
- Travellers: passing through on foot, counting the miles, sleeping where they can.
- Adventurers: bounties, and going where others will not. They come back or they do not.
- Villagers: the settlement itself - the well, the walls, gossip, weather, neighbours, keeping things going.

## The strange, as ordinary background

The valley has an old strangeness in it. People treat it as weather - something to allow for, not to marvel at.
The Foldscar is the place that frightens them. Stones that stand in threes, marks in rock, things that were touched
and did not die. Nothing in a chat line explains it, and no chat line gives anyone power over it.

## Creatures (write them in lower case)

wolves, a boar, stags and hinds, hares, a fox, hens and a cock, a hound, spiders in the caves, husks that keep the
barrow. Two grazers have proper names and may be named: **Barrowback** and **Fellhorn**; the hen is an **Ashcomb**.
Say "wolves", never a capitalised creature name that is not one of those three.

## Weather

fair, overcast, rain, fog, wind. A front lasts four to twelve hours. Wet ground, risen ditches, mist in the low
ground, wind off the crags, a cold that gets into wool.

## Calendar

**Reckoning** is the year. **Hollow Day** is the day at a Reckoning's end that belongs to no tally. That is all a chat
line knows about the calendar: no months, no weekdays, no dates, no seasons of your own invention.

## Named people: others may speak of them, never as them

Kera Voss and Durn Skell (smiths), Renn Vale (steward), Sel Arien (archivist and surveyor), Tavar Orr (guide),
Berrin Garth (keeps the Long Lodge), Mara Ling (keeps the well), Tam Crowden (holds the granary). A line may refer to
a trade - "the smith", "the steward", "the surveyor" - and should prefer the trade. **No line may say where a named
person is or what they are doing at any hour**: they keep their own routines and the game moves them. Do not use their
signature lines; several are in the banned list.

## Two factions

**the Wayhold** - the waystation's watch and stewardship. **the Long Survey** - the surveyors and scholars working the
old workings and the Foldscar. A line may mention either as a body of people, not as an authority with new powers.

## What a line must not do

- No new proper noun of any kind: no invented place, person, people, tavern, hill, god, war, kingdom, coin, month,
  festival, song or beast.
- No numbers that state a world fact: no prices, distances, populations, tolls, taxes, years, army sizes.
- No claim about an event the game does not track ("the bridge went out last spring", "the mine closed"). Weather,
  work, the road, the bog, the crags, the barrow, and what the speaker can see are safe.
- No speech that addresses the player, offers a job, or gives directions.
- No quoted speech from a named person, and nothing in quotation marks at all.
